§ 10.01 TITLE OF CODE.
   All ordinances of a permanent and general nature of the town, as revised,
codified, rearranged, renumbered, and consolidated into component codes,
titles, chapters, and sections, shall be known and designated as the “Wingate
Code,” for which designation “code of ordinances,” “codified ordinances,” or
“code” may be substituted. Code title, chapter, and section headings do not
constitute any part of the law as contained in the code.
§ 10.02 INTERPRETATION.
   Unless otherwise provided herein, or by law or implication required, the
same rules of construction, definition, and application shall govern the
interpretation of this code as those governing the interpretation of state law.
§ 10.03 APPLICATION TO FUTURE ORDINANCES.
   All provisions of Title I compatible with future legislation shall apply to
ordinances hereafter adopted amending or supplementing this code unless
otherwise specifically provided.
§ 10.04 CONSTRUCTION OF CODE.
   (A)   This code is a codification of previously existing laws, amendments
thereto, and newly enacted laws. Any previously existing law or amendment
thereto reenacted by this code shall continue in operation and effect as if it
had not been repealed by this code. All rules and regulations adopted under
laws reenacted in this code shall remain in full force and effect unless
repealed or amended subsequent to the enactment of this code.
   (B)   Any appropriation repealed and reenacted by this code is continued
only for the period designated in the original enactment of that appropriation.
   (C)   The numerical order and position of sections in this code does not
resolve a conflict between two or more sections.
   (D)   Any irreconcilable conflict between sections shall be resolved by
reference to the dates that the sections were originally enacted. The section
most recently enacted supersedes any conflicting section or division.
   (E)   All references within a section of this code to any section of
previously existing laws refer to the numbers in the original enactment.
   (F)   (1)   The numerical designations and descriptive headings assigned to
the various titles, chapters, subchapters, or sections of this code, as
originally enacted or as added by amendment, are not law, and may be altered by
the compilers of this or any subsequent codification, in any official
publication, to more clearly indicate its content.
      (2)   These descriptive headings are for organizational purposes only and
do not affect the meaning, application, or construction of the law they
precede.
      (3)   Each note following a section of this code is for reference
purposes only and is not a part of the section.
   (G)   All references to any section of this code refer to all subsequent
amendments to that section, unless otherwise provided.
(I.C. 1-1-1-5)
§ 10.05 RULES OF INTERPRETATION; DEFINITIONS.
   (A)   Rules of interpretation. This code shall be construed by the following
rules unless the construction is plainly repugnant to the legislative intent or
context of the provision.
      (1)   Words and phrases shall be taken in their plain, ordinary, and
usual sense. Technical words and phrases having a peculiar and appropriate
meaning in law shall be understood according to their technical import.
      (2)   Words imputing joint authority to three or more persons shall be
construed as imputing authority to a majority of the persons, unless otherwise
declared in the section giving the authority.
      (3)   Where a section requires an act to be done which, by law, an agent
or deputy may perform in addition to the principal, the performance of the act
by an authorized deputy or agent is valid.
      (4)   Words denoting the masculine gender shall be deemed to include the
feminine and neuter genders; words in the singular shall include the plural,
and words in the plural shall include the singular; the use of a verb in the
present tense shall include the future, if applicable.
   (B)   Definitions. For the purpose of this code of ordinances, the following
definitions shall apply unless the context clearly indicates or requires a
different meaning.
      CLERK-TREASURER. The Clerk-Treasurer of the Town.
      COUNCIL. The Town Council.
      COUNTY. Montgomery County, Indiana.
      HIGHWAY. Includes bridges, roads, and streets, unless otherwise expressly
provided.
      MONTH. One calendar month.
      PERSON. Extends to and includes person, persons, firm, corporation,
copartnership, trustee, lessee, receiver, and bodies politic. Whenever used in
any clause prescribing and imposing a penalty, the terms PERSON or WHOEVER as
applied to any unincorporated entity shall mean the partners or members
thereof, and as applied to corporations, the officers, or agents thereof.
      PRECEDING and FOLLOWING. When referring to sections or divisions in this
code, refer to the sections or divisions next following or next preceding that
in which the words occur, unless some other section is designated.
      TOWN. The Town of Wingate, Indiana.
      TOWNSHIP. Coal Creek Township, Indiana.
      WRITTEN and IN WRITING. Include printing, lithographing, or other modes
of representing words and letters. Where the written signature of a person is
required, the terms mean the proper handwriting of the person, or the person’s
mark.
      YEAR. One calendar year, unless otherwise expressly provided.
(I.C. 1-1-4-5)
§ 10.06 SEVERABILITY.
   (A)   If any section of this code now enacted or subsequently amended or its
application to any person or circumstances is held invalid, the invalidity does
not affect other sections that can be given effect without the invalid section
or application.
   (B)   Except in the case of a section or amendment to this code containing a
nonseverability provision, each division or part of every section is severable.
If any portion or application of a section is held invalid, the invalidity does
not affect the remainder of the section unless:
      (1)   The remainder is so essentially and inseparably connected with and
so dependent upon the invalid provision or application that it cannot be
presumed that the remainder would have been enacted without the invalid
provision or application; or
      (2)   The remainder is incomplete and incapable of being executed in
accordance with the legislative intent without the invalid provision or
application.
   (C)   This section applies to every section of this code regardless of
whether a section was enacted before or after the passage of this code.
   (D)   The repeal of a section or amendment stating that the provisions of a
chapter, subchapter, or section are severable as provided in division (B) above
does not affect the operation of division (B) with respect to that chapter,
subchapter, or section.
(I.C. 1-1-1-8)
§ 10.07 REFERENCE TO OTHER SECTIONS.
   Whenever in one section reference is made to another section hereof, the
reference shall extend and apply to the section referred to as subsequently
amended, revised, recodified, or renumbered unless the subject matter is
changed or materially altered by the amendment or revision.
§ 10.08 REFERENCE TO OFFICES; NAME DESIGNATIONS.
   (A)   Reference to offices. Reference to a public office or officer shall be
deemed to apply to any office, officer, or employee of this municipality
exercising the powers, duties, or functions contemplated in the provision,
irrespective of any transfer of functions or change in the official title of
the functionary.
   (B)   Name designations. Whenever any ordinance or resolution of the Council
refers to any board, bureau, commission, division, department, officer, agency,
authority, or instrumentality of any government, and that name designation is
incorrectly stated; on the effective date of that ordinance or following the
effective date, the rights, powers, duties, or liabilities placed with that
entity are or were transferred to a different entity; then the named board,
bureau, commission, department, division, officer, agency, authority, or
instrumentality, whether correctly named in the ordinance at its effective date
or not, means that correctly named entity, or the entity to which the duties,
liabilities, powers, and rights were transferred.
(I.C. 1-1-6-1)
§ 10.09 ERRORS AND OMISSIONS.
   (A)   If a manifest error is discovered, consisting of the misspelling of
any words; the omission of any word or words necessary to express the intention
of the provisions affected; the use of a word or words to which no meaning can
be attached; or the use of a word or words when another word or words was
clearly intended to express the intent, the spelling shall be corrected and the
word or words supplied, omitted, or substituted as will conform with the
manifest intention, and the provisions shall have the same effect as though the
correct words were contained in the text as originally published.
   (B)   No alteration shall be made or permitted if any question exists
regarding the nature or extent of the error.
§ 10.10 REASONABLE TIME.
   (A)   In all cases where an ordinance requires an act to be done in a
reasonable time or requires reasonable notice to be given, REASONABLE TIME OR
NOTICE shall be deemed to mean the time which is necessary for a prompt
performance of the act or the giving of the notice.
   (B)   The time within which an act is to be done, as herein provided, shall
be computed by excluding the first day and including the last. If the last day
be a Saturday, Sunday, or a state holiday, it shall be excluded.
§ 10.11 REPEAL OR MODIFICATION OF CODE SECTION.
   When a section of this code is repealed which repealed a former section or
law adopted prior to the enactment of this code, the former section or law is
not revived unless it so expressly provides. The repeal of any section shall
not extinguish or release any penalty, forfeiture, or liability incurred under
the section, unless the repealing section so expressly provides. The section
shall be treated as still remaining in force for the purposes of sustaining any
proper action or prosecution for the enforcement of the penalty, forfeiture, or
liability.
(I.C. 1-1-5-1)
§ 10.12 LIMITATION PERIODS.
   The running of any period of limitations or any requirement of notice
contained in any law, whether applicable to civil causes or proceedings, or to
the prosecution of offenses, or for the recovery of penalties and forfeitures,
contained in a law repealed and reenacted by this code, shall not be affected
by the repeal and reenactment; but all suits, proceedings, and prosecutions for
causes arising or acts committed prior to the effective date of this code may
be commenced and prosecuted with the same effect as if this code had not been
enacted.
Statutory reference:
   Periods of limitation, see I.C. 1-1-1-7
§ 10.13 ORDINANCES UNAFFECTED.
   All ordinances of a temporary or special nature and all other ordinances
pertaining to subjects not embraced in this code shall remain in full force and
effect unless herein repealed expressly or by necessary implication.
§ 10.14 ORDINANCES WHICH AMEND OR SUPPLEMENT CODE.
   (A)   If the legislative body shall desire to amend any existing chapter or
section of this code, the chapter or section shall be specifically repealed and
a new chapter or section, containing the desired amendment, substituted in its
place.
   (B)   Any ordinance which is proposed to add to the existing code a new
chapter or section shall indicate, with reference to the arrangement of this
code, the proper number of the chapter or section. In addition to an indication
thereof as may appear in the text of the proposed ordinance, a caption or title
shall be shown in concise form above the ordinance.
§ 10.15 SECTION HISTORIES; STATUTORY REFERENCES.
   (A)   As histories for the code sections, the specific number and passage
date of the original ordinance, and amending ordinances, if any, are listed
following the text of the code section. Example:
(Ord. 10, passed 5-13-1960; Ord. 15, passed 1-1-1970; Ord. 20, passed 1-1-1980;
Ord. 25, passed 1-1-1985)
   (B)   (1)   If a statutory cite is included in the history, this indicates
that the text of the section reads substantially the same as the statute.
Example:
(I.C. 36-5-2-2)
      (2)   If a statutory cite is set forth as a “statutory reference”
following the text of the section, this indicates that the reader should refer
to that statute for further information. Example:
         § 39.01 PUBLIC RECORDS AVAILABLE.
            This municipality shall make available to any person for inspection
or copying all public records, unless otherwise exempted by state law.
         Statutory reference:
            Inspection of public records, see I.C. 5-14-3-1 et seq.
   (C)   If a section of this code is derived from the previous code of
ordinances of the city published and subsequently amended, the previous code
section number shall be indicated in the history by “(Prior Code, §      ).”
§ 10.16 PRESERVATION OF PENALTIES, OFFENSES, RIGHTS, AND LIABILITIES.
   All offenses committed under laws in force prior to the effective date of
this code shall be prosecuted and remain punishable as provided by those laws.
This code does not affect any rights or liabilities accrued, penalties
incurred, or proceedings begun prior to the effective date of this code. The
liabilities, proceedings, and rights are continued; punishments, penalties, or
forfeitures shall be enforced and imposed as if this code had not been enacted.
In particular, any agreement granting permission to utilize highway rights-of-
way, contracts entered into, or franchises granted, the acceptance,
establishment, or vacation of any highway, and the election of corporate
officers shall remain valid in all respects, as if this code had not been
enacted.
§ 10.99 GENERAL PENALTY.
   (A)   Any person, firm, or corporation who violates any provision of this
code for which another penalty is not specifically provided shall, upon
conviction, be subject to the following:
      (1)   A fine not exceeding $2,500 for the first violation; and
      (2)   A fine not exceeding $7,500 for second and subsequent violations,
except for violations of ordinances regulating traffic and parking.
   (B)   A separate violation shall be deemed committed upon each day during
which a violation occurs or continues.
Statutory reference:
   Authority, see I.C. 36-1-3-8(a)(10)

§ 30.01 DEFINITIONS.
   For the purpose of this chapter, the following definitions apply unless the
context clearly indicates or requires a different meaning.
   COUNCIL. The Town Council of the Town of Wingate, Indiana.
   OPEN DOOR LAW. I.C. 5-14-1.5 as the same exists on the effective date of
this code or as may be amended, recodified, or reenacted hereafter.
   PRESIDENT. President of the Town Council of Wingate, Indiana.
(Prior Code, § 2-1-1)
§ 30.02 QUORUM AND MEETINGS.
   (A)   Except as otherwise provided by statute, a majority of the Council
shall meet in regular session at 6:00 p.m. on the second Tuesday of each month
at the Town Hall, and at such other times and places as the Council may from
time to time determine and select by motion or upon special call by the
President, as hereinafter provided.
   (B)   The annual meeting for adoption of the annual budget and tax rates
shall be held at either a special or regular meeting as may be convenient and
as required by law.
   (C)   The Council shall conduct all other business of the town.
(Prior Code, § 2-2-1)
§ 30.03 SPECIAL MEETING.
   (A)   The President, on his or her own initiative, may, at any time, call a
special meeting of the Council.
   (B)   If, in the judgment of the President, the purpose of the meeting is to
meet an immediate emergency confronting the town or a part thereof, the meeting
may be called by oral contact with the other members of the Council and the
Clerk-Treasurer, with further oral notice to the news media as required under
the Open Door Law, being I.C. 5-14-1.5-1 et seq. Such oral contact or notice
may be held in person, or via telephonic or electronic means.
   (C)   If the purpose of such meeting is not an immediate emergency under
division (B) above, such meeting may be called by giving the other members of
the Council and the Clerk-Treasurer, and the news media, 48 hours’ oral or
written notice prior to the convening of such meeting. Such oral contact or
notice may be held in person, or via telephonic or electronic means.
(Prior Code, § 2-2-2)
§ 30.04 OPEN DOOR LAW.
   All meetings shall be conducted in accordance with the Open Door Law, being
I.C. 5-14-1.5-1 et seq., including, but not limited to, the following
requirements.
   (A)   Notices. Notices as required by the Open Door Law shall be given to
the Crawfordsville Journal Review at Crawfordsville, Indiana. In the event of a
special meeting, the same notice shall be given to the media as shall be given
members of the Council.
   (B)   Date. The news media identified in division (A) above shall be
notified of the regular meetings yet to be held by the Council as of the
effective date of this code within ten days of that effective date, and shall
thereafter be notified annually of such regular meetings, not later than
January 15.
   (C)   Agenda. The meetings of the Council will generally not be subject to a
written agenda, but governed by the general order of business as set forth
hereafter; however, in the event any meeting shall be convened or held upon the
basis of a written agenda, a copy thereof shall be furnished to the news media
in accordance with the Open Door Law.
(Prior Code, § 2-2-3)
§ 30.05 ACTION OF COUNCIL, COMMITTEES, RULES OF ORDER.
   Business coming before the Council shall be acted upon directly by the
Council, but may be referred to an appropriate committee by a majority
affirmative vote of the Council members then present, such committee to report
action on the matter or matters at such times, places, and manners as the
Council may direct. Such standing or special committees shall exist and
function as may from time to time be established by the majority affirmative
vote of the Council members then present, and the President of the Council
shall be an ex officio member of all such committees. All procedures, motions,
or conduct of meetings of the Council shall be governed by Robert’s Rules of
Order, unless the same be suspended by an affirmative majority vote of the
members then present.
(Prior Code, § 2-2-5)
§ 30.06 PRESENTATION OF OLD AND NEW BUSINESS.
   When the roll is called for presentation of old or new business, each member
of the Council shall present to the Council all old or new business which he or
she wishes the Council to consider at the meeting. Any business not presented
on such roll call may be placed before the Council by the majority affirmative
vote of the members then present.
(Prior Code, § 2-2-6)
§ 30.07 GENERAL RULES FOR CONDUCT OF BUSINESS.
   The following general rules shall apply to the conduct of all business at a
meeting of the Council.
   (A)   Limit on discussion. The President may reasonably limit debate or
discussion on any issue or matter before the Council, and his or her decision
to limit the same shall be final.
   (B)   Public comment. Any member of the public shall be permitted to address
the Council on any subject properly placed before the Council for discussion,
but only after all members of the Council have made their own addresses,
remarks, comments, or presentations regarding the subject. Any member of the
public shall be permitted to address the Council on any matter pertaining to
the town or town business, whether or not a member of the Council has presented
the same for consideration, but only after all other business of the meeting
has been concluded.
   (C)   Record of attendance. At all meetings of the Council, the Clerk-
Treasurer shall secure a written roll call of all persons present by roll-call
or by a sign-in book, indicating thereon whether such persons appear as
officials of the town, employees of the town, agents of the town, or members of
the public, and in the latter case, whether the member of the public has
identified a specific purpose or purposes of such attendance.
(Prior Code, § 2-2-7)
§ 30.08 ORGANIZATION.
   At the first meeting of the Council after December 31, but in no event after
January 15 in the succeeding year, the Council shall convene for the purpose of
organizing itself for the year as follows.
   (A)   President. At the organizational meeting, the Council shall elect from
its membership a President for a period of one year, or the remainder of his or
her term of office as a member of the Council, whichever is the shorter period.
The results of such election shall be made part of the official minutes of the
meeting.
   (B)   Other officers. The Council may elect from its membership such
additional officers as it deems convenient to the conduct of its business, and
may do so either at the organization meeting or any other meeting.
(Prior Code, § 2-2-8)
§ 30.09 COMPENSATION.
   The Council members receive such compensation for their service as may be
provided by ordinance.
(Prior Code, § 2-3-5)
§ 30.10 TERMS.
   The Town Board Council elected in 2023 shall serve terms of three years,
with terms ending in 2026. Council members elected thereafter shall serve terms
of four years, with each election taking place in an even-numbered year.
(Res. 2023-02, passed 8-8-2023)

§ 31.01 NEPOTISM POLICY.
   (A)   (1)   The town finds that it is necessary and desirous to adopt a
policy of conduct with regard to nepotism in the employment with the town and
in contracting with the town in order to continue to be able to provide local
government services to its residents and to comply with the new laws effective
July 1, 2012, known as I.C. 36-1-20.2 and I.C. 36-1-21, respectively.
      (2)   On September 1, 2015 the town shall have a nepotism and a
contracting with a unit policy that complies with the minimum requirements of
I.C. 36-1-20.2 (hereinafter “nepotism policy”) and I.C. 36-1-21 (hereinafter
“contracting with a unit by a relative policy”), and implementation of those
policies will begin on that date.
      (3)   The town nepotism policy is hereby established effective September
1, 2015, by adopting the minimum requirements and provisions of I.C. 36-1-20.2,
and including all future supplements and amendments thereto which become law
from time to time, and making them a part hereof as if fully set out herein. A
copy of I.C. 36-1-20.2 in effect on July 1 is attached to the resolution from
which this section is derived for reference.
      (4)   The town contracting with a unit by a relative policy is hereby
established effective September 1, 2015, by adopting the minimum requirements
and provisions of I.C. 36-1-21, and including all future supplements and
amendments thereto which become law from time to time, and making them a part
hereof as if fully set out herein. A copy of the I.C. 36-1-21 in effect on July
1 is attached to the resolution from which this section is derived for
reference.
      (5)   The town finds that both I.C. 36-1-20.2 and I.C. 36-1-21
specifically allow a unit to adopt requirements that are “more stringent or
detailed” and that more detailed requirements are advisable.
      (6)   The town therefore finds that a single member of the legislative
body cannot act for the body to make work assignments, compensation,
grievances, advancement, or a performance evaluation without prior authority of
a majority of that body, and therefore, without such authority by the majority
of that body he or she will not be in the direct line of supervision.
      (7)   The town finds that a single member of a governing body with
authority over employees in the town cannot act for that governing body to make
work assignments, determine compensation, hear or file grievances, and evaluate
advancement or performance without prior authority of a majority of that body
when a statute provides that a majority is needed to act, and therefore,
without such authority by the majority, the single member will not be in the
direct line of supervision.
      (8)   All elected and appointed officials and employees of the town are
hereby directed to cooperate fully in the implementation of the policies
created by this section and demonstrating compliance with these same policies.
      (9)   (a)   Failure to abide by or cooperate with the implementation,
compliance, and certifications connected with the nepotism policy is a
violation of town employment rules and may result in the transfer or
discipline, including termination, of an employee or some other curative
action.
         (b)   An elected or appointed official of the town who fails to abide
by or cooperate with the implementation, compliance, and certifications of
either the nepotism policy may be subject to further action allowed by law.
      (10)   Failure to abide by or cooperate with the implementation,
compliance, and certifications connected with the contracting with unit by a
relative policy is a violation and may result in the discipline, including
termination, of an employee or some other curative action. An elected or
appointed official of the town who fails to abide by or cooperate with the
implementation, compliance, and mandated certifications of the contracting with
unit by a relative policy may be subject to further action allowed by law.
      (11)   (a)   The policies created by this section are hereby directed to
be implemented by any of the following actions:
            1.   Posting a copy of this section in its entirety in at least one
of the locations in the town where it posts employer posters or other notices
to its employees;
            2.   Providing a copy of this section to its employees and elected
and appointed officials;
            3.   Providing or posting a notice of the adoption of this section;
or
            4.   Any such other action or actions that would communicate the
policies established by this section to its employees and appointed officials.
         (b)   Upon taking any of these notification actions, these policies
are deemed implemented by the town.
      (12)   Two copies of I.C. 36-1-20.2 and I.C. 36-1-21, and as supplemented
or amended, shall be kept on file in the office of the Clerk-Treasurer for the
town for public inspection as may be required by I.C. 36-1-5-4.
   (B)   All other provisions of the town code and employment policies not in
conflict with this section shall remain in full force and effect.

§ 31.02 COMPENSATION.
   Annually, on or before December 31, the Council shall enact an ordinance
fixing, for the coming calendar year, the wages, salaries, or other
compensation of all officials and employees of the town. Such annual
compensation ordinance need not be printed as or physically incorporated into
this code, but shall be of the same force and effect as if printed as or
physically incorporated herein. If the Council fails to timely enact such an
ordinance, compensation for the succeeding year shall remain the same as for
the year in which the meeting for passage of the ordinance was required to have
been timely held.
(Prior Code, § 2-10-1)
§ 31.03 TRAVEL EXPENDITURES.
   (A)   Officials and employees of the town are authorized and directed to
attend meetings, conferences, training sessions and other gatherings in the
furtherance of town business at which his or her attendance is required by law
or deemed highly beneficial to the business of the town, and which have been
approved by the Council at a Council meeting.
   (B)   For the purpose of this section, the following definition applies
unless the context clearly indicates or requires a different meaning.
      OFFICIAL.
         (a)   Any person elected to an office of the town and qualified to

**** CAPITAL ASSET POLICY ****
§ 32.20 PURPOSE.
   This capital asset policy will be effective for capital assets beginning in
2023. The purpose of this policy is to facilitate the preparation of financial
statements in conformity with generally accepted accounting principles.
(Res. 2023-01, passed 5-16-2023)
§ 32.21 CLASSIFICATION OF ASSETS.
   For the purpose of this subchapter, the following definition applies unless
the context clearly indicates or requires a different meaning.
   CAPITAL ASSETS. Personal and real property used in the operations of the
town that have an expected estimated useful life beyond a single period.
CAPITAL ASSETS are to include any item that falls into one of the following
categories:
      (1)   Land;
      (2)   Building and building improvements;
      (3)   Improvements other than buildings;
      (4)   Machinery and equipment;
      (5)   General infrastructure (roads, bridges, rights-of-way, water/sewer
lines, storm drains, street lighting, sidewalks); or
      (6)   Construction in progress.
(Res. 2023-01, passed 5-16-2023)
§ 32.22 CAPITALIZATION THRESHOLDS.
   (A)   To be considered a capital asset for financial reporting purposes, an
item (other than land) must be at or above the capitalization threshold and
have a unit historical cost of $50,000 or more.
   (B)   The capitalization threshold for the following classes of assets shall
be:
      (1)   Machinery, equipment, and vehicles: $5,000;
      (2)   Buildings and building improvements: $100,000;
      (3)   Improvements other than buildings: $50,000; and
      (4)   Infrastructure: $250,000.
   (C)   With regard to improvements and buildings and general infrastructure,
a capital outlay must be significant and increase capacity, increase
efficiency, or extend the asset’s estimated useful life beyond the original
expectation.
   (D)   A change in capacity increases the level of service provided by the
asset. A change in efficiency increases the level of service but, without
increasing the size of the asset or the change, maintains the same level of
service at a lower cost. For example, an addition to a building provides
increased square footage, hence, the capacity is increased and the capital
outlay is capitalized. Widening a road with additional lanes increases capacity
and hence, the capital outlay is capitalized. An extended estimated useful life
involves a significant alteration, structural change, or improvement.
   (E)   While substantial repairs and renovations will be reviewed for
potential capitalization, it is anticipated that most will be expenses in the
current year. These expenses often merely restore the asset to the original
service potential but do not necessarily improve the asset.
   (F)   All land, including rights-of-way, is capitalized at the time of
acquisition, regardless of historical costs or fair value if donated.
(Res. 2023-01, passed 5-16-2023)
§ 32.23 PROSPECTIVE REPORTING.
   Capital assets are recorded at historical cost, which includes any ancillary
charges necessary to place the asset into its intended location and condition
for use. Ancillary charges include, for example, freight and transportation
charges, site preparation costs, and professional fees. Engineering costs
(internal and external) include related preliminary project and environmental
studies; project estimating, design, and planning (drawings and
specifications); and construction engineering, construction management,
construction inspection, and project payment. Donated capital assets are
recorded at their estimated fair value at the time of acquisition.
(Res. 2023-01, passed 5-16-2023)
§ 32.24 RETROACTIVE REPORTING.
   (A)   When actual historical cost source data was unavailable, estimated
historical cost was developed utilizing a normal costing approach. With this
method of estimating historical cost, a current replacement cost was
ascertained.
   (B)   An appropriate cost index (including Consumer Price Index and Federal
Highway Price Trends) corresponding to an estimated date of acquisition/
construction was then applied to “deflate” the replacement cost to an estimated
historical cost.
(Res. 2023-01, passed 5-16-2023)
§ 32.25 ESTIMATED USEFUL LIVES OF DEPRECIABLE ASSETS.
   (A)   Capital assets have estimated useful lives extending beyond a single
reporting period (one year) and are depreciated using the straight-line method
with no allowance for salvage value.
   (B)   The estimated useful lives currently used were developed with the
input of knowledgeable staff and reflect our government’s experience with these
assets.
      (1)   Land and rights-of-way:
         (a)   Buildings and improvements to buildings: 50 years; and
         (b)   Machinery and equipment: five years.
      (2)   Vehicles:
         (a)   Automobiles: five years;
         (b)   Light trucks: eight years; and
         (c)   Heavy trucks: 15 years.
      (3)   Improvements other than buildings:
         (a)   Paths and trails: 15 years;
         (b)   Parking lots: 15 years;
         (c)   Retaining walls: 15 years;
         (d)   Lighting systems: 15 years;
         (e)   Plazas and pavilions: 25 years;
         (f)   Fencing: 15 years; and
         (g)   Water features: 15 years.
      (4)   General infrastructure:
         (a)   Roads: 50 years;
         (b)   Bridges: 75 years;
         (c)   Water/sewer lines: 50 years;
         (d)   Street lighting: 25 years;
         (e)   Storm drains: 50 years; and
         (f)   Sidewalks: 15 years.
(Res. 2023-01, passed 5-16-2023)
§ 32.26 DEPRECIATION METHOD/CONVENTION.
   Depreciation will be calculated using the straight-line method and full-year
convention. No salvage value or residual value will be recognized.
(Res. 2023-01, passed 5-16-2023)
§ 32.27 RETIREMENTS.
   (A)   Retirements apply to all capital assets, including land, buildings,
machinery and equipment, vehicles, and general infrastructure. When an asset is
disposed of, scrapped, sold, subject to demolition, and the like, it is to be
removed from the property record and the appropriate reduction will be made to
historical cost, accumulated depreciation, and net book value amounts.
   (B)   Retirements will reflect the actual historical cost of the asset when
the amount is ascertainable. When historical cost is not ascertainable, an
estimated historical cost will be determined.
(Res. 2023-01, passed 5-16-2023)
§ 32.28 RESPONSIBILITY FOR PROPERTY RECORD MAINTENANCE.
   (A)   The Clerk-Treasurer will ensure that reporting for capital assets is

the capital asset report will be updated annually to reflect improvements,
additions, retirements, and transfers and to reflect the new annual capital
asset balance for financial reporting purposes and the annual and accumulated

   (B)   Day-to-day stewardship of property is the expressed responsibility of

§ 50.001 PURPOSE; ESTABLISHMENT.
   (A)   Purpose. This chapter establishes “Wingate Utilities” as a unified
entity to manage the town’s water, wastewater, and storm water services for
households, commercial users, and industrial users, consisting of the Water
Utility, Wastewater Utility, and Storm Water Utility. It delineates the
separate operational and financial responsibilities of each department,
mandates the use of town-provided septic tanks and town-managed graywater
lagoon systems for wastewater management, establishes permit and connection
fees for wastewater services, acknowledges a storm water fee as established by
Ordinance 2015-2, and recognizes that water and wastewater rates are set
through periodic rate ordinances.
   (B)   Establishment.
      (1)   Creation. The town hereby establishes Wingate Utilities as the
municipal utility department responsible for managing the town’s water,
wastewater, and storm water services for households, commercial users,
industrial users, institutional users, and governmental users.
      (2)   Structure. Wingate Utilities shall consist of three systems:
         (a)   The Water Department, responsible for the operation,
maintenance, and management of the town’s water supply, treatment, and
distribution systems;
         (b)   The Wastewater Department, responsible for the operation,
maintenance, and management of the town’s wastewater collection, treatment, and
disposal systems, including septic tanks and graywater lagoon systems; and
         (c)   The Storm Water Department, responsible for the operation,
maintenance, and management of the town’s storm water drainage and treatment
systems, also referred to as the Department of Storm Water Management.
      (3)   Governance. Wingate Utilities shall be governed by the Town Council
of Wingate, which serves as the directors of the Department of Storm Water
Management for the Storm Water Department, in accordance with I.C. 8-1.5-3-
3 and I.C. 8-1.5-5-4. The Town Council may appoint a utility board or designate
an administrator to oversee the operations of the water and wastewater systems.
      (4)   Unified branding. The water, wastewater, and storm water systems
shall operate under the unified name “Wingate Utilities” for all public-facing
communications, billing, and administration purposes, while maintaining
separate operational and financial records as required by this chapter.
(Ord. 2025, passed - -2025)
§ 50.002 DEFINITIONS.
   For the purpose of this chapter, the following definitions apply unless the
context clearly indicates or requires a different meaning.
   BOD (BIOCHEMICAL OXYGEN DEMAND). The quantity of oxygen utilized in the
biochemical oxidation of organic matter under standard laboratory procedure in
five days at 20°C, expressed in milligrams per liter.
   BUILDING SEWER. The extension from the building drain to the public sewer or
other place of disposal.
   COMBINED SEWER. A sewer receiving both surface runoff and sewage.
   COMMERCIAL USER. Any non-governmental, non-residential, or non-institutional
user operating within the town’s jurisdictional boundaries that utilizes water,
wastewater, or storm water services, discharging less than the equivalent of
2,500 gallons per day of sanitary waste.
   COMPATIBLE POLLUTANT. Biochemical oxygen demand, suspended solids, pH, and
fecal coliform bacteria, plus additional pollutants identified in the NPDES
permit if the publicly owned treatment works was designed to treat such
pollutants, and in fact, does remove such pollutants to a substantial degree
(generally 40% or greater).
   GARBAGE. Solid wastes from the domestic and commercial preparation, cooking
and dispensing of food, and from the handling, storage, and sale of produce.
   GOVERNMENTAL USER. Any non-commercial and non-industrial user of the town’s
sewage works owned and operated by the town.
   GRAYWATER LAGOON SYSTEM. A system designed to treat and dispose of graywater
(wastewater from sources such as laundry, showers, sinks, or commercial
processes, excluding toilet waste) through a lagoon or pond, constructed and
managed by Wingate Utilities’ Wastewater Department.
   HOUSEHOLD. Any residential dwelling unit, including single-family homes,
duplexes, or other residential structures, occupied or intended for occupancy,
primarily discharging normal domestic sewage.
   INCOMPATIBLE POLLUTANT. Any pollutant which is not a compatible pollutant as

§ 50.004 PROHIBITED DISCHARGE.
   (A)   No person shall place, deposit, discharge, or allow or cause the
placement, depositing, or discharge of any of the following substances,
compounds, items, mixtures, or things into any sanitary sewer, regardless of
whether the same be described below, or be in combination with one another,
with water, or with any other substance:
      (1)   Any storm water, surface water, ground water, roof runoff,
subsurface drainage, uncontaminated cooling water, or unpolluted industrial
process water. Storm water and all other unpolluted drainage shall be
discharged to such sewers as are specifically designated to be storm sewers, to
natural outlets, or in such other manner as approved by the Superintendent.
Industrial cooling water or unpolluted process waters may be discharged, on
approval of the Superintendent, to a storm sewer, combined sewer, or natural
outlet;
      (2)   Gasoline, benzene, naphtha, fuel oil, or any other flammable or
explosive liquid, solid, or gas;
      (3)   Any toxic or poisonous solid, liquid, or gas in sufficient
quantity, either singly or by interaction with any other substance or waste, to
pose a substantial risk of:
         (a)   Injuring persons or animals;
         (b)   Interfering with any sewage treatment process;
         (c)   Creating a public nuisance; or
         (d)   Creating any hazard in the receiving waters of the sewage
treatment plant.
      (4)   Any waters, wastes, or substances having pH lower than five and
one-half, or having any other corrosive property capable of causing obstruction
to the flow in sewers or other interference with the proper operation of the
sewage works. Such waters, wastes, or substances include, but are not limited
to, ashes, cinders, sand, mud, straw, shavings, metal, glass, rags, feathers,
tar, plastics, wood, unground garbage, whole blood, manure, hair and flesh,
paper or plastic dishes, paper or plastic cups, paper or plastic milk
containers, whether or not the same are whole or have passed through any device
used to grind, tear, or cut; and/or
      (5)   Any of the following substances, materials, waters, or wastes if it
appears likely in the opinion of the Superintendent the same can: harm the
sewers, sewage treatment process, or equipment; have an adverse effect on the
receiving stream, or; otherwise endanger life, limb, public property, or
constitute a nuisance:
         (a)   Any liquid or vapor having a temperature higher than 150°F or
65°C;
         (b)   Any fats, wax, grease, or oils, whether emulsified or not, in
excess of 100 mg/l or containing substances which may solidify or become
viscous at temperatures between 32°F and 150°F or 65°C;
         (c)   Any garbage that has not been properly shredded by a device
equipped with a motor of at least three-fourths horsepower (0.76 hp metric).
The installation and connection of such device shall be subject to the review
and approval of the Superintendent;
         (d)   Any strong acid iron pickling wastes, or concentrated plating
solutions whether or not neutralized;
         (e)   Any iron, chromium, copper, zinc, and similar objectionable or
toxic substances; or wastes exerting a chlorine requirement to such an extent
that the same as received in the composite sewage at the sewage treatment works
exceeds the limits established by the Superintendent for such materials;
         (f)   Any phenols or other taste- or odor-producing substances, in
concentrations exceeding limits which may be established by the Superintendent
as necessary, after treatment of the composite sewage, to meet the requirements
of any law governing such discharge to the receiving waters;
         (g)   Radioactive wastes or isotopes of such half-life or
concentration as may exceed limits established by the Superintendent in
compliance with applicable law;
         (h)   Any waters or wastes having pH in excess of nine or lower than
five;
         (i)   Materials which exert or cause unusual concentrations of inert
solids, suspended solids, or dissolved solids including, but not limited to
Fuller’s earth, lime slurries, lime residues, sodium chloride, and sodium
sulfate;
         (j)   Materials which exert or cause excessive discoloration,
including, but not limited to, dye wastes and vegetable tanning solutions;
         (k)   Materials which exert or cause unusual BOD, chemical oxygen
demand, or chlorine requirements so as to constitute a significant load on the
sewage treatment works;
         (l)   Materials which exert or cause unusual volume of flow or
concentration of wastes constituting “slugs” as defined herein;
         (m)   Waters or wastes containing substances which are not amenable to
treatment or reduction by the sewage treatment processes employed by the Water
Department, or which are amenable thereto only by rendering sewage treatment
plant effluent unable to meet the requirements of other agencies having
jurisdiction over discharge to the receiving waters; and
         (n)   Anything exceeding the standards applicable thereto now or
hereafter established by any government agency having jurisdiction over the
activities or any activity of the Water Department.
   (B)   In forming his or her opinion as to the acceptability of these wastes,
the Superintendent will give consideration to such factors as the quantities of
subject wastes in relation to flows and velocities in the sewers, materials of
construction of the sewers, nature of the sewage treatment process, capacity of
the sewage treatment plant, degree of the treatability of wastes in the sewage
treatment plant, and other pertinent factors.

§ 50.006 PRIVATE SEWAGE DISPOSAL SYSTEM.
   (A)   Where required. Where a public sanitary or combined sewer is not
available for connection under §
50.005, the owner of any house, building, or property used for human occupancy,
employment, recreation, or any other purpose, situated within the town is
required at his or her own expense to install suitable toilet facilities
therein, and to connect such facilities to a private sewage disposal system
complying with the provisions of this section.
   (B)   Permit application. Before commencement of construction of a private
sewage disposal system, the owner shall first obtain a written permit issued by
the Town Council. The application for such permit shall be made on a form
prescribed by the Council, and shall be accompanied by any plans,
specifications, and other information as may hereafter be deemed necessary by
the Town Council. A tap-on fee of $2,000 shall be paid to the town at the time
of the application is filed.
   (C)   Permit effective.
      (1)   A permit for a private sewage disposal system shall not become
effective until the installation is completed to the satisfaction of the
Superintendent. He or she shall be allowed to inspect the work at any stage of
construction and, in any event, the applicant for the permit shall notify the
Superintendent when the work is ready for final inspection and before any
underground portions are covered.
      (2)   The inspection shall be made within three days of the receipt of
notice by the Superintendent, provided that if the third day is a Saturday,
Sunday, or legal holiday, the inspection shall occur on the following day that
is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday.
   (D)   Specifications. The type, capacities, location and layout of a private
sewage disposal system shall comply with all recommendations of the State Board
of Health. No permit shall be issued for any private sewage disposal system
employing subsurface soil absorption facilities where the area to be served
thereby is less than 15,000 square feet, or such other area as may be required
by any applicable law. No septic tank or cesspool shall discharge to any
natural outlet.
   (E)   Obligation to connect. At such time as a public sewer becomes
available to a property served by a private sewage disposal system, the owner
of the property shall at his or her own expense make a direct connection to the
public sewer in compliance with this section. Further, the owner shall at his
or her own expense ensure that any septic tanks, cesspools, and similar private
sewage disposal facilities are thereafter abandoned, cleaned of sludge, and
filled with clean bank-run gravel or dirt.
   (F)   Operation. The owner shall at his or her sole expense operate and
maintain the private sewage disposal facilities in a sanitary manner at all
times.
   (G)   Health Officer. No statement contained in this section shall be
construed to interfere with any additional requirements that may be imposed by
a public official charged with the duty of preserving, promoting, or protecting
public health.

§ 50.008 SEWER BUILDING PERMITS.
   (A)   Permit required. No person not employed by the town or the Water
Department shall uncover, make any connection with or opening into, use, alter,
or disturb any public sewer or appurtenance thereof without first obtaining a
written permit issued by the Water Department.
   (B)   Classes of permits. There shall be two classes of sewer building
permits: a Class I permit, for residential and commercial service; and a Class
II permit, for service to establishments producing industrial wastes. In either
case, the owner or his or her agent shall make application on a form prescribed
by the Town Council, together with any plans, specifications, or other
information considered pertinent in the judgment of the Superintendent. A
permit and inspection fee of $100 for a Class I permit and $200 for a Class II

made.

and connection of a sewer under a Class I or Class II permit shall be borne by
the owner, who shall save, hold harmless, and indemnify the town from any loss
or damage that may directly or indirectly be occasioned by the owner’s work
with respect to the sewer.
   (D)   Tap-on fee. Any person performing work under a Class I or Class II
permit and who requests a new connection to the sewage works shall, at the time
to making such request, pay a tap-on fee as determined by the Town Council, and
the fee shall be available for inspection.

provided for every building. If one building stands at the rear of another on

building and the whole considered as one building/sewer combination.
   (F)   Found sewers. Sewer connections, pipes, or appurtenances found or
already existing on property may be used by new buildings only when the same
are determined, on examination by the Water Department, to meet all the
requirements of this section and the laws.

§ 50.009 REGULATIONS FOR SEWER CONNECTION.
   (A)   Compliance with codes and regulations.
      (1)   The size, slope, alignment, materials of construction of a sewer,
and the methods to be used in excavating, placing of the pipe, jointing,
testing, and backfilling the trench, shall all conform to the requirements of
the building and plumbing code or other requirements of the building and
plumbing code or other applicable rules and regulations of the town.
      (2)   In the absence of specific laws or in amplification thereof, the
materials and procedures set forth in appropriate specifications of the latest
edition of the A.S.T.M and S.P.C.F. Manual of Practice shall apply.
   (B)   Elevation. Whenever possible, the sewer shall be brought to the
building at an elevation below the basement floor. In all buildings in which
any building drain is too low to permit gravity flow to the public sewer,
sanitary sewage carried by such building drain shall be lifted by an approved
means and discharged to the building sewer.
   (C)   Downspouts. No person shall make connection of roof downspouts,
exterior foundation drains, areaway drains, or other sources of surface runoff
or ground water to a building sewer or building drain which in turn is
connected directly or indirectly to a public sanitary sewer.
   (D)   Conformity to law. The connection of the sewer into the public sewer
shall conform to the requirements of the building and plumbing code or other
applicable rules and regulations of the town, or the procedures set forth in
appropriate specifications of the latest edition to the A.S.T.M. and the
W.P.C.F. Manual of Practice. All such connections shall be made gas-tight and
water-tight. Deviation from the prescribed procedures and materials must be
approved by the Superintendent before installation.
   (E)   Inspection. The applicant for the Class I or Class II permit shall
notify the Water Department when the sewer is ready for inspection and
connection to the public sewer. The connection shall be made under the
supervision of the said Superintendent or his or her representative.
   (F)   Excavation. All excavations for building sewer installation shall be
adequately guarded with barricades and lights so as to protect the public from
hazard. Streets, sidewalks, parkways, and other public property disturbed in
the course of the work shall be restored in a manner satisfactory to the town.
   (G)   Connection dates. All services for connection to be performed by the
Water Department shall be done on Monday through Friday, inclusive, and it is
specifically determined that no such work shall be performed on any Saturday,
Sunday, or legal holiday.

§ 50.019 FUNDS.
   (A)   Operation and Maintenance Fund. There is hereby created a fund to be
known as the “Operation and Maintenance Fund,” to which fund there shall be
credited as of the last day of each calendar month a sufficient amount of the
revenues of the sewage works so that the balance in said fund shall be
sufficient to pay the expenses of operation, repair and maintenance for the
then next succeeding two calendar months. The moneys credited to this fund
shall be used for the payment of the reasonable and proper operation, repair,
and maintenance expenses of the sewage works on a day-to-day basis, but none of
the moneys in such fund shall be used for depreciation, replacements,
improvements, extensions, or additions. Any balance in said fund in excess of
the expected expenses of operation, repair, and maintenance for the next
succeeding month may be transferred to the Sewage Works Sinking Fund if
necessary to prevent a default in the payment of principal or interest on the
outstanding bonds.
(Prior Code, § 2-8-22)
   (B)   Sinking Fund. There is hereby created a sinking fund for the payment
of the principal of and interest on revenue bonds which by their terms are
payable form the revenue of the sewage works, and the payment of any fiscal
agency charges in connection with the payment of bonds and interest coupons,
which fund shall be designated the “Sewage Works Sinking Fund.” There shall be
set aside and deposited in said Sinking Fund, as available, and as hereinafter
provided a sufficient amount of the net revenues of said sewage works (defined
as gross revenues exclusive of recovered grant amounts form industrial users as
required by the Environmental Protection Agency, after deduction only for the
payment of the reasonable expenses of operation, repair and maintenance) to
meet the requirements of the Bond and Interest Account and of the Debt Service
Reserve Account hereby created in said Sewage Works Sinking Fund. Such payment
shall continue until the balance in the Bond and Interest Account, plus the
balance in the Debt Service Reserve Account hereinafter created, equal the
principal of and interest of all of the then outstanding bonds to the final
maturity thereof.
(Prior Code, § 2-8-23)
   (C)   Improvement Fund. After meeting the requirements of the Operation and
Maintenance Fund, and the Sewage Works Sinking Fund, any excess revenues may be
transferred or credited to a fund designated the “Sewage Works Improvement
Fund,” and said fund shall be used for improvements, replacements, additions,
and extensions of the sewage works. Moneys in the Sewage Works Improvement Fund
shall be transferred to the Sewage Works Sinking Fund, if necessary, to prevent
a default in the payment of principal and interest on the then outstanding
bonds or, if necessary, to eliminate any deficiencies in credits to or minimum
balance in the Debt Service Reserve Account of the Sewage Works Sinking Fund.
(Prior Code, § 2-8-24)
   (D)   Separate accounts. The Sewage Works Sinking Fund shall be deposited in
and maintained as a separate bank account or accounts apart from all other bank
accounts of the town. The Operation and Maintenance Fund and the Sewage Works
Improvement Fund may be maintained in a single bank account, or accounts, but
such bank account, or accounts, shall likewise be maintained separate and apart
from all other bank accounts of the town and apart from the Sewage Works
Sinking Fund bank account or accounts. All moneys deposited in said bank
accounts shall be deposited, held, and secured as public funds in accordance
with the public depository laws of the state; provided that moneys therein may
be invested in obligations in accordance with the applicable laws as amended or
supplemented, and in the event of such investment the income therefrom shall
become a part of the funds invested and shall be used only as provided in this
subchapter.
(Prior Code, § 2-8-25)
§ 50.020 OPERATIONAL RESPONSIBILITIES.
   (A)   Water Department.
      (1)   Functions. The Water Department shall manage the sourcing,
treatment, storage, and distribution of potable water to all users within the
town’s jurisdictional boundaries, in compliance with State Department of Health
standards and other applicable regulations.
      (2)   Infrastructure. The Water Department shall maintain all water-
related infrastructure, including treatment plants, distribution lines, and
meters, separate from wastewater and storm water infrastructure.
      (3)   Service standards. The Water Department shall ensure continuous,
safe, and reliable water service, with regular testing and reporting as
required by state and federal law. Service levels shall accommodate the varying
demands of all user classes.
   (B)   Wastewater Department.
      (1)   Functions. The Wastewater Department shall manage the collection,
treatment, and disposal of wastewater from all users, including the provision
and maintenance of septic tanks and the construction, operation, and
maintenance of graywater lagoon systems, in compliance with IDEM standards (327
I.A.C. § 3).
      (2)   Septic tank requirements.
         (a)   All users shall utilize a septic tank provided by the Wastewater
Department for the collection of wastewater solids, unless granted an exemption
under §
50.025(D).
         (b)   No user shall install or maintain a privately owned septic tank
unless granted an exemption by the Town Council.
         (c)   The Wastewater Department shall install septic tanks in
compliance with IDEM and State Department of Health regulations, subject to the
user’s payment of a permit fee as outlined in this section.
         (d)   For commercial, industrial, and institutional users, septic
tanks shall be sized and designed to accommodate the specific wastewater volume
and characteristics of the user, as determined by the Wastewater Department.
      (3)   Graywater lagoon systems.
         (a)   All users shall utilize a graywater lagoon system constructed
and managed by the Wastewater Department for the treatment and disposal of
graywater, unless granted an exemption under §
50.025(D).
         (b)   Graywater lagoon systems receive only graywater, with septic
tanks handling toilet waste and other solids. For commercial, industrial, and
institutional users, the composition of graywater shall comply with IDEM
discharge standards.
         (c)   The Wastewater Department shall design and construct graywater
lagoon systems to meet the specific needs of each user type, ensuring capacity
for varying wastewater volumes.
      (4)   Sewer connection requirements.
         (a)   Users with property abutting a public sanitary or combined sewer
within 100 feet of the property line shall connect to the public sewer within
90 days of receiving official notice, at their own expense, in accordance with
the town’s building and plumbing codes.
         (b)   Where a public sewer is unavailable, users shall install a
private sewage disposal system, subject to a permit and approval by the
Wastewater Department, in compliance with State Board of Health
recommendations.
         (c)   Private sewage disposal systems shall not discharge to any
natural outlet and must comply with minimum area requirements (e.g., 15,000
square feet for subsurface soil absorption facilities).
         (d)   When a public sewer becomes available, users with private
systems shall connect to the public sewer and abandon private facilities,
cleaning, and filling them with gravel or dirt at their own expense.
      (5)   Prohibited discharges.
         (a)   No user shall discharge storm water, surface water, groundwater,
roof runoff, uncontaminated cooling water, or unpolluted industrial process
water into sanitary sewers. Such discharges shall be directed to storm drains
or natural outlets with approval from the Wastewater Department Superintendent.
         (b)   Prohibited substances include flammable or explosive materials,
toxic or poisonous substances, corrosive materials (pH <5.5 or >9.0), high-
temperature liquids (>150°(F)), excessive grease or oils (>100 mg/l),
unshredded garbage, and other materials that may harm sewers or treatment
processes, as specified in the town’s municipal code.
      (6)   Pretreatment and control.
         (a)   Industrial users discharging incompatible pollutants or
excessive waste shall install pretreatment or flow-equalization facilities,
subject to the Superintendent’s approval, at the user’s expense.
         (b)   Grease, oil, and sand interceptors shall be installed by users
when deemed necessary by the Superintendent, except for private dwellings, and
maintained at the user’s expense.
         (c)   Industrial users may be required to install control manholes for
monitoring and sampling, constructed and maintained at the user’s expense.
         (d)   The Wastewater Department shall maintain all wastewater-related
infrastructure, including septic tanks, graywater lagoon systems, public
sewers, and associated piping, separate from water and storm water
infrastructure.
      (7)   Storm Water Department.
         (a)   Functions. The Storm Water Department , also referred to as the
Department of Storm Water Management, shall manage the collection, conveyance,
and treatment of storm water runoff from all users, in compliance with IDEM
storm water regulations (327 I.A.C. § 15), the town’s Municipal Separate Storm
Sewer System (MS4) program, if applicable, and the Storm Water Study Plan
adopted under Resolution 2015-1.
         (b)   Infrastructure. The Storm Water Department shall maintain all
storm water-related infrastructure, including drainage ditches, culverts,
retention ponds, and treatment facilities, separate from water and wastewater
infrastructure.
         (c)   Compliance. The Storm Water Department shall ensure compliance
with federal and state storm water regulations, including the Clean Water Act
and IDEM’s National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES) permits,
with specific attention to industrial users’ storm water discharge
requirements.
      (8)   Permit and connection fees for wastewater.
         (a)   Septic tank permit fee. All users shall be responsible for a
one-time permit fee to cover the cost of the septic tank provided and installed
by the Wastewater Department. The fee amount shall be determined by the Town
Council based on user type and system requirements and published in the town’s
fee schedule. The fee must be paid prior to installation.
         (b)   Private sewage disposal permit fee. Users installing private
sewage disposal systems where public sewers are unavailable shall obtain a
permit from the Wastewater Department, with a fee of $50, payable at the time
of application.
         (c)   Sewer connection permit fees. Users connecting to public sewers
shall obtain a sewer building permit, with fees of $100 for Class I
(residential and commercial) and $200 for Class II (industrial), payable to the
Clerk-Treasurer at the time of application.
         (d)   Tap-on fee. Users requesting a new connection to the public
sewer system shall pay a tap-on fee, as determined by the Town Council and
published in the town’s fee schedule, to cover inspection and local capital
costs.
         (e)   Coordination. The water, wastewater, and storm water systems
shall coordinate as needed to ensure efficient utility services, particularly
for users with high water, wastewater, or storm water demands, but each
department shall maintain distinct operational protocols and staff
responsibilities.

Department, Wastewater Department, and Storm Water Department, as required by
I.C. 8-1.5-3-4 and I.C. 8-1.5-5-6, to prevent cross-subsidization and ensure

   (C)   Wastewater Department funding.
      (1)   The Wastewater Department shall be funded primarily through
wastewater service fees, septic tank permit fees, sewer connection permit fees,
and tap-on fees charged to all users, as established by periodic rate
ordinances adopted by the Town Council in accordance with I.C. 8-1.5-3-8, and
published in the town’s fee schedule.
      (2)   The Wastewater Department shall cover the costs of septic tank
inspection, pumping, repairs, and replacements, as well as the construction,

to user misuse or negligence.
      (3)   A Sewage Works Sinking Fund shall be maintained to meet bond
obligations and debt service requirements, as required by the town’s municipal
code.
      (4)   An Operation and Maintenance Fund and a Sewage Works Improvement
Fund may be maintained in a single bank account, separate from the Sewage Works

replacements, and improvements.
      (5)   Excess revenues, after meeting Operation and Maintenance and
Sinking Fund requirements, shall be transferred to the Sewage Works Improvement
Fund for improvements, replacements, additions, or extensions of the wastewater
system.
   (D)   Storm Water Department Funding.
      (1)   The Storm Water Department shall be funded primarily through storm
water fees charged to all users, as established by Ordinance 2015-2 and set by
the Town Council under I.C. 8-1.5-5-7. Fees may vary based on impervious
surface area or other criteria specified in the town’s fee schedule.
      (2)   Additional funding may include grants, bonds, or other revenues
designated for storm water management, including funds from the Community
Development Block Grant referenced in Resolution 2015-1.
   (E)   Reporting. Financial reports for each department, including complete
operating income and expense statements, shall be maintained and made available
for public inspection, with copies provided to bondholders within 90 days of
the fiscal year’s end, in accordance with the town’s municipal code.

§ 50.022 MAINTENANCE RESPONSIBILITIES OF THE WASTEWATER DEPARTMENT.
   (A)   Septic tanks.
      (1)   Inspection. The Wastewater Department shall inspect each user’s
septic tank at least once every three to five years to assess condition and
ensure proper functioning, with inspection frequency adjusted for commercial,
industrial, and institutional users based on wastewater volume or regulatory
requirements.
      (2)   Pumping. The Wastewater Department shall arrange and cover the cost
of pumping each septic tank as needed, based on inspection findings, but no
less frequently than every three to five years.
      (3)   Repairs and replacements. If an inspection reveals the need for
repairs or replacement, the Wastewater Department shall undertake such actions
at no direct cost to the user, unless damage is due to misuse or negligence as
determined by the town.
      (4)   Scheduling. The Wastewater Department shall notify users in writing
at least 30 days in advance of scheduled inspections or pumping activities.
Users shall provide reasonable access to the septic tank.
   (B)   Graywater lagoon systems.
      (1)   Inspection. The Wastewater Department shall inspect the graywater
lagoon system at least once every three to five years to ensure proper
functioning and environmental safety, with inspection frequency adjusted for
commercial, industrial, and institutional users as needed.
      (2)   Maintenance. The Wastewater Department shall perform necessary
maintenance, including desilting, vegetation control, and structural repairs,
at no direct cost to the user, unless damage is due to misuse or negligence as
determined by the town.
      (3)   Scheduling.
         (a)   The Wastewater Department shall notify users in writing at least
30 days in advance of scheduled inspections or maintenance activities.
         (b)   Users shall provide reasonable access to the system.
   (C)   Public sewers.
      (1)   The Wastewater Department shall maintain public sanitary and
combined sewers, ensuring they remain free of blockages and compliant with IDEM
standards.
      (2)   Inspections and repairs shall be conducted as needed, with users
responsible for maintaining private building sewers to the point of connection
with the public sewer.

§ 50.024 USER RESPONSIBILITIES.
   (A)   Access. All users (households, commercial users, industrial users,
institutional users, and governmental users) shall ensure that septic tanks,
graywater lagoon systems, public sewers, and storm water infrastructure are
accessible for inspection, maintenance, and repair activities conducted by
Wingate Utilities.
   (B)   Proper use.
      (1)   Users shall avoid disposing of non-biodegradable materials,
chemicals, or excessive grease into septic tanks, graywater lagoon systems, or
public sewers, as these may cause damage or system failure.
      (2)   Users shall ensure that only graywater is directed to graywater
lagoon systems, and that toilet waste and other solids are directed to septic
tanks or public sewers. Commercial, industrial, and institutional users shall
comply with IDEM regulations for wastewater discharge, including pretreatment
requirements.
      (3)   Users shall prevent unauthorized discharges (e.g., pollutants,
flammable materials, or high-temperature liquids) into sanitary sewers or storm
water systems, in compliance with IDEM and Clean Water Act regulations.
      (4)   Industrial users shall install and maintain pretreatment
facilities, interceptors, or control manholes as required by the Wastewater
Department Superintendent, at their own expense.
   (C)   Reporting. Users shall promptly report any signs of system failure,
such as septic tank backups, lagoon overflows, sewer blockages, storm water
flooding, or pollution, to Wingate Utilities within 24 hours.
   (D)   Payment of fees.
      (1)   Users shall pay the septic tank permit fee, sewer connection permit
fees, and tap-on fee as required under §
50.020 prior to installation or connection.
      (2)   Users shall pay water and wastewater rates as established by
periodic rate ordinances adopted by the Town Council and published in the

      (3)   Users shall pay the storm water fee as established by Ordinance
2015-2 and set by the Town Council, in accordance with I.C. 8-1.5-5-7.
   (E)   Compliance. Users shall comply with all directives from Wingate
Utilities related to the use and maintenance of water, wastewater, and storm
water systems, including any additional IDEM or federal requirements applicable
to commercial, industrial, or institutional users.
   (F)   Private sewage disposal. Users with private sewage disposal systems
shall operate and maintain them in a sanitary manner at their own expense,
until a public sewer becomes available, at which point they shall connect to
the public sewer and abandon private systems.

the environment.
      (2)   Approval. Exemptions shall be granted at the discretion of the Town
Council after review by Wingate Utilities designated health or environmental
officer. Approved exemptions may require periodic inspections at the user’s

§ 50.027 WATER METER TAMPERING.
   (A)   Definitions. For the purpose of this section, the following
definitions shall apply unless the context clearly indicates or requires a
different meaning.
      TAMPERING. Any unauthorized act that interferes with the proper
functioning, accuracy, or security of a water meter or water meter pit,
including, but not limited to, altering, damaging, bypassing, removing, or
connecting to the meter or pit without Utility approval.
      TOWN COUNCIL. The governing body of the Town of Wingate, acting as the
Utility Board pursuant to this chapter.
      UTILITY. The Combined Water and Wastewater Utility of the Town of
Wingate, as established by this chapter.
      WATER METER. A device installed by the Utility to measure the volume of
water usage for billing purposes, including any associated components such as
registers, sensors, or remote reading equipment.
      WATER METER PIT. The structure or enclosure, including lids, covers, and
associated fittings, that houses the water meter to protect it from damage and
provide access for maintenance and reading.
   (B)   Prohibition of tampering.
      (1)   Tampering prohibited. It shall be unlawful for any person, firm, or
entity to tamper with, alter, damage, bypass, remove, or otherwise interfere
with any water meter or water meter pit owned or operated by the utility,
except as expressly authorized by the utility.
      (2)   Unauthorized connections. No person, firm, or entity shall make any
connection to the utility’s water system through or around a water meter or
water meter pit without prior written approval from the utility.
      (3)   Access restrictions. Only authorized utility personnel or agents
shall access, repair, replace, or modify water meters or water meter pits.
Unauthorized access to or entry into a water meter pit is prohibited.
      (4)   Prohibited acts. Prohibited acts include, but are not limited to:
         (a)   Breaking or removing seals, locks, or covers on water meters or
water meter pits;
         (b)   Installing devices to bypass or manipulate water meter readings;
         (c)   Damaging or obstructing access to water meters or water meter
pits;
         (d)   Diverting water to avoid metering or billing; and
         (e)   Altering or relocating a water meter or water meter pit without
utility approval.
   (C)   Enforcement and penalties.
      (1)   Violation notice. Upon discovery of tampering or unauthorized
activity involving a water meter or water meter pit, the Utility Manager or
designated personnel shall issue a written notice of violation to the
responsible party, specifying the nature of the violation and required
corrective actions.
      (2)   Service disconnection. The Utility may disconnect water service to
any property where tampering is confirmed, pursuant to I.C. 8-1.5-3-4,
following written notice and a 1ten-day opportunity to cure the violation.
Reconnection shall be contingent upon payment of all fines, repair costs, and
reconnection fees.
      (3)   Civil remedies. The Utility may pursue civil action to recover
damages, costs, and attorney fees resulting from tampering or unauthorized use
of the water system, as authorized by I.C. 8-1.5-3-8.
      (4)   Criminal referral. In cases of willful or repeated tampering, the
utility may refer the matter to local law enforcement for investigation and
prosecution under applicable state criminal statutes.
   (D)   Administration.
      (1)   Utility manager oversight. The Utility Manager, appointed under
this chapter, or any other person as designated by the Town Council, shall be
responsible for enforcing this section, including investigating suspected
violations, issuing notices, and recommending penalties to the Town Council.
      (2)   Appeals. Any person or entity receiving a violation notice or
penalty may appeal to the Town Council within 15 days of receiving the notice.
The Town Council shall hold a hearing within 30 days and issue a final
decision, which shall be binding unless overturned by a court of competent
jurisdiction.
      (3)   Clerk-Treasurer role. The Clerk-Treasurer shall oversee the
collection of fines and fees imposed under this section.
   (E)   Notification and education.
      (1)   Public notification. The utility shall notify customers of this
ordinance through billing inserts, the town’s website, or other public
communication methods within 60 days of its adoption.
      (2)   Customer education. The utility shall provide information to
customers about the importance of protecting water meters and water meter pits
and the consequences of tampering, including potential fines and service
disruptions.

§ 50.036 DEFINITIONS.
   For the purpose of this subchapter, the following definitions apply unless
the context clearly indicates or requires a different meaning.
   AIR GAP. The unobstructed vertical distance through the free atmosphere
between the lowest opening from any pipe or faucet supplying water to a tank,
plumbing fixture, or other devise or flood level rim of said vessel. An
approved AIR GAP shall be at least double the diameter of the supply pipe,
measured vertically, above the overflow rim of the vessel; and in no case less
than one inch.
   APPROVED. Accepted by Wingate Utilities as meeting applicable specification
stated or cited in this subchapter, or as suitable for the purposed use.
   AUXILIARY WATER SUPPLY. Any water supply on or available to the premises
other than the utility’s approved potable water supply. These AUXILIARY WATERS
may include water from another utility’s public potable water supply or any
natural source(s) such as a well, spring, river, stream, and the like, or “used
waters” or “industrial fluids.” These waters may be contaminated or polluted,
or they may be objectionable and constitute an unacceptable water source over
which the water utility does not have sanitary control.
   BACKFLOW. The reversal of the normal flow of water caused by either
backpressure or backsiphonage.
   BACKPRESSURE. The flow of water or other liquids, mixtures, or substances
under pressure into the distribution pipes of a potable water supply system
from any source or source other than the intended source.
   BACKSIPHONAGE. The flow of water or other liquids, mixtures, or substances
into the distribution pipes of a potable water supply caused by the reduction
of pressure in the potable water supply system.
   BACKFLOW PREVENTER. An approved assembly or means designed to prevent
backflow.
   CONTAMINATION. An impairment of the quality of the potable water by sewage,
industrial fluids or waste liquids, compounds, or other materials to a degree
which creates an actual or potential hazard to the public health through
poisoning or through the spread of disease.
   CROSS-CONNECTION. Any physical connection or arrangement of piping or
fixtures between two otherwise separate piping systems, one of which contains
potable water and the other non-potable water or industrial fluids of
questionable safety, through which, or because of which, backflow may occur
into the potable water system.
   CROSS-CONNECTION CONTROL BY CONTAINMENT. The installation of an approved
backflow assembly at the water service connection to any customer’s premises
where it is physically and economically unfeasible to find and permanently
eliminate or control all actual or potential cross-connections within the
customer’s water system; or it shall mean the installation of an approved
backflow prevention assembly on the service line leading to and supplying a
portion of a customer’s water system where there are actual or potential cross-
connections which cannot be effectively eliminated or controlled at the point
of cross-connection.
   CROSS-CONNECTION CONTROLLED. A connection between a potable water system and
a non-potable water system with an air gap or approved backflow prevention
assembly installed and maintained so that it will continuously afford the
protection commensurate with the degree of hazard.
   DEGREE OF HAZARD. An evaluation of the potential risk and the adverse effect
of the hazard upon the potable water system.
   DOUBLE CHECK VALVE ASSEMBLY. An assembly of two independently operating,
approved check valves with resilient seated shut-off valves on each end of the
check valves, plus properly located resilient seated test cocks for testing of
each check valve. The entire ASSEMBLY shall meet the design and performance
specifications as determined by a laboratory and field evaluation program
resulting in an approval by a recognized and Wingate Utilities approved testing
agency for backflow prevention assemblies. To be approved, these ASSEMBLIES
must be readily accessible for in-line testing and maintenance. Confined space
installations will not be accepted.
   HEALTH HAZARD. Any condition, devise, or practice in the water supply system
and its operation which could create or, in the judgment of Wingate Utilities,
may create a danger to the health and wellbeing of the water customer.
   INDUSTRIAL FLUIDS SYSTEM. Any system containing a fluid or solution, which
may be chemically, biologically, or otherwise contaminated or polluted in a
form or concentration such as would constitute a health, system, pollutional,
or plumbing hazard if introduced into an approved water supply. This may
include, but not be limited to: polluted or contaminated waters; all types of
process waters and “used water” originating from the public potable water
system which may have deteriorated in sanitary quality; chemicals in fluid
form; plating acids and alkalizes, circulating cooling waters connected to an
open cooling water and/or cooling towers that are chemically or biologically
treated or stabilized with toxic substances; contaminated natural waters such
as wells, springs, streams, rivers, irrigation canal or systems, and the like;
oils, gases, glycerin, paraffin, caustic and acid solutions and other liquid
and gaseous fluids used in industrial or other purposes or for firefighting
purposes.
   NON-POTABLE WATER. Water which is not safe for human consumption or which is
of questionable potability.
   PLUMBING HAZARD. A plumbing type cross-connection in a customer’s potable
water system that has not been properly protected by an approved air gap or
approved backflow prevention assembly.
   POLLUTION. The presence of any foreign substance (organic, inorganic, or
biological) in water which tends to degrade its quality so as to constitute a
hazard or impair the usefulness or quality of the water to a degree which does
not create an actual hazard to the public health but which does adversely and
unreasonably affect such waters for domestic use.
   POLLUTIONAL HAZARD. An actual or potential threat to the physical water
system but which could constitute a nuisance or be aesthetically objectionable
or could cause damage to the system or its appurtenances, but would not be
dangerous to health.
   POTABLE WATER. Any water which, according to recognized standards, is safe
for human consumption.
   REDUCED PRESSURE PRINCIPLE ASSEMBLIES. An assembly of two independently
acting approved check valves together with a hydraulically operating,
mechanically independent differential pressure relief valve located between the
check valves and, at the same time, below the first check valve, the unit shall
include properly located resilient seated test cocks and resilient seated shut-
off valves at each end of the ASSEMBLY. The entire ASSEMBLY shall meet the
design and performance specifications as determined by a laboratory and a field
evaluation program resulting in approval by a recognized and Wingate Utilities
approved testing agency for backflow prevention assemblies. The ASSEMBLY shall
operate to maintain the pressure in the zone between the check valves at an
acceptable level less than the pressure on the public water supply side of the
ASSEMBLY. At cessation of normal flow, the pressure between the two check
valves shall be less than the pressure on the public water supply side of the
ASSEMBLY. In case of leakage of either of the check valves, the differential
relief valve shall operate to maintain the reduced pressure in the zone between
the check valves by discharging to the atmosphere. When the inlet pressure is
two pounds per square inch or less, the relief valve shall open to the
atmosphere. To be approved, these ASSEMBLIES must be readily accessible for in-
line testing and maintenance and be installed in a location where no part of
the assembly will be submerged. Confined space installations will not be
approved.
   SUPERINTENDENT. The Superintendent of the Wingate Water Utilities, or his or
her designee, who is vested with the authority and responsibility for the
impletion of an effective cross-connection control program and for the
enforcement of provisions of this subchapter.
   SYSTEM HAZARD. An actual or potential threat of severe damage to the
physical properties of the public potable water system or the consumer’s
potable water system or of a pollution or contamination which would have a
protracted effect on the quality of the potable water in the system.
   USED WATER. Any water supplied by a water utility from a public potable
water system after it has passed through the point of delivery and is no longer
under the sanitary control of the water utility.
   WATER SERVICE CONNECTION. The terminal end of a service connection from the
public potable water system, i.e., where the water utility loses jurisdiction
and sanitary control over the water at its point of delivery to the customer’s
water system. If a meter is installed at the end of the service connection,
then the service connection shall mean the downstream end of the meter,
provided the meter is installed outside of the building; i.e., in a pit or
vault. If the meter is installed inside a building, then the water utility’s
jurisdiction terminates at the downstream end of the outside shut-off valve to
the water service. There should be no unprotected takeoffs from the water
service line ahead of any meter or ahead of any backflow prevention assembly.
Service connection shall also include WATER SERVICE CONNECTION from a fire
hydrant and all other temporary or emergency WATER SERVICE CONNECTIONS from the
public potable water system.
(Ord. 2018-03, passed 5-8-2018)

§ 50.042 TYPE OF PROTECTIVE ASSEMBLY.
   The type of protective assembly required shall depend upon the degree of
hazard which exists as follows.
   (A)   In the case of any premises where there is an auxiliary water supply,
the public water systems shall be protected by an approved air gap separation
or an approved reduced pressure principle backflow prevention assembly.
   (B)   In the case of any premises where there is water or a substance that
would be objectionable but not hazardous to health, if introduced into the
public water system, the public water system shall be protected by an approved
reduced pressure principle backflow assembly.
   (C)   In the case of any premises where there is any material dangerous to
health such as to create an actual or potential hazard to the public water
system, the public water system shall be protected by an approved air gap
separation or an approved reduced principle backflow prevention assembly.
Examples of premises where these conditions will exist include, but are not
limited to, sewage treatment plants, sewage pumping stations, chemical
manufacturing plants, hospitals, mortuaries, and planting plants.
   (D)   In the case of any premises where there are uncontrolled cross-
connections, either actual or potential, the public water system shall be
protected by an approved air gap separation or an approved reduced pressure
principle backflow prevention assembly at the service connection.
   (E)   In the case of any premises where, because of security requirements or
other prohibitions or restrictions, it is impossible or impractical to make a
complete in-plant cross-connection survey, the public water system shall be
protected against backflow from the premises by either an approved air gap
separation or an approved reduced pressure principle backflow prevention
assembly on each service to the premises.
   (F)   In the case of any premises having a lawn irrigation system, the
public water system shall be protected by an approved pressure vacuum breaker
backflow prevention assembly or an approved reduced pressure principle backflow
prevention assembly.
   (G)   In the case of any premises having a fire service line, the public
water system shall be protected by an approved double detector check valve
assembly.
   (H)   All other premises not covered in divisions (A) to (G) above shall be
required to install a non- testable dual check valve assembly for the
protection of the public water system.
   (I)   Existing installations are not exempt from current rules and
regulations in this subchapter.
(Ord. 2018-03, passed 5-8-2018)

SEPTIC TANK AND GRAYWATER LAGOON SYSTEMS
§ 50.060 PURPOSE.
   (A)   This subchapter establishes regulations for the mandatory installation
and use of town-provided septic tanks in all households within the town for the
collection of wastewater solids and authorizes the use of town-managed
graywater lagoon systems for the treatment and disposal of household
wastewater.
   (B)   It also outlines the town’s responsibilities for the provision,
inspection, and maintenance of both septic tanks and graywater lagoon systems,
and establishes a permit fee for septic tanks to be paid by homeowners.
(Ord. passed - -)
§ 50.061 DEFINITIONS.
   For the purpose of this subchapter, the following definitions shall apply
unless the context clearly indicates or requires a different meaning.
   GRAYWATER LAGOON SYSTEM. A town-managed system designed to treat and dispose
of household graywater (wastewater from sources such as laundry, showers, and
sinks, excluding toilet waste) through a lagoon or pond, constructed and
maintained by the Town of Wingate in accordance with state and local
regulations.
   HOUSEHOLD. Any residential dwelling unit, including single-family homes,
duplexes, or other residential structures, occupied or intended for occupancy.
   SEPTIC TANK. A watertight, underground tank designed to collect and hold
solid waste from household wastewater, provided and installed by the Town of
Wingate.
   TOWN. The Town of Wingate, Indiana, or its designated representatives.
(Ord. passed - -)
§ 50.062 MANDATORY SEPTIC TANK USE.
   (A)   Requirement. All households within the jurisdictional boundaries of
the town shall utilize a septic tank provided by the town for the collection
and holding of wastewater solids.
   (B)   Prohibition. No household shall install, maintain, or use a privately
owned septic tank unless granted a specific exemption by the Town Council under
§
50.066.
   (C)   Installation. The town shall provide and install a septic tank for
each household, subject to compliance with this ordinance and payment of the
permit fee described in division (D) below. Installation shall comply with all
applicable state and local regulations, including those of the State Department
of Health.
   (D)   Permit fee. Homeowners shall be responsible for a one-time permit fee
to cover the cost of the septic tank provided and installed by the town. The
fee amount shall be determined by the Town Council and published in the town’s
fee schedule. The fee must be paid prior to septic tank installation, and no
installation shall proceed until the fee is received by the town.
(Ord. passed - -)
§ 50.063 TOWN-MANAGED GRAYWATER LAGOON SYSTEMS.
   (A)   Authorization. All households shall utilize a town-managed graywater
lagoon system for the treatment and disposal of household graywater, unless
granted an exemption under §
50.066.
   (B)   Construction and installation. The town shall maintain a graywater
lagoon system to ensure compliance with standards set by the State Department
of Health and other applicable regulations.
   (C)   Connection to septic tank. Graywater lagoon systems shall only receive
graywater separated from septic tank wastewater (blackwater). The septic tank
shall handle all toilet waste and other solids.
(Ord. passed - -)
§ 50.064 TOWN MAINTENANCE RESPONSIBILITIES FOR SEPTIC TANKS.
   (A)   Inspection. The town shall conduct inspections of each household’s
septic tank at least once every three to five years to assess the condition of
the tank and ensure proper functioning.
   (B)   Pumping. The town shall arrange and cover the cost of pumping each
septic tank as needed, based on inspection findings, but no less frequently
than every three to five years.
   (C)   Scheduling. The town shall notify property owners in writing at least
30 days in advance of scheduled inspections or pumping activities. Property
owners shall provide reasonable access to the septic tank for these purposes.
   (D)   Repairs and replacements. If an inspection reveals the need for
repairs or replacement of a septic tank, the town shall undertake such actions
at no direct cost to the property owner, unless damage is due to misuse or
negligence by the property owner as determined by the town.
(Ord. passed - -)
§ 50.065 PROPERTY OWNER RESPONSIBILITIES.
   (A)   Access. Property owners shall ensure that both the septic tank and
graywater lagoon system are accessible for inspection, maintenance, and repair
activities conducted by the town.
   (B)   Proper use.
      (1)   Property owners shall avoid disposing of non-biodegradable
materials, chemicals, or excessive grease into the septic tank or graywater
lagoon system, as these may cause damage or system failure.
      (2)   Property owners shall ensure that only graywater (e.g., from
laundry, showers, sinks, and the like) is directed to the graywater lagoon
system, and that toilet waste and other solids are directed to the septic tank.
   (C)   Reporting. Property owners shall promptly report any signs of system
failure, such as septic tank backups, lagoon overflows, odors, or surface
pooling, to the town’s designated contact within 24 hours.
   (D)   Compliance. Property owners shall comply with all town directives
related to the use and maintenance of septic tanks and graywater lagoon
systems.
(Ord. passed - -)
§ 50.066 VIOLATION AND ENFORCEMENT.
   (A)   Violations. Failure to comply with any provision of this ordinance,
including denying access for inspection or maintenance, improper use of the
septic tank or graywater lagoon system, or failure to report system issues,
shall constitute a violation.
   (B)   Notice of violation. The town shall issue a written notice of
violation, providing the property owner with 30 days to correct the violation
before imposing penalties, unless immediate action is required to protect
public health or safety.
(Ord. passed - -)
§ 50.067 EXEMPTIONS.
   (A)   Application. A property owner may apply for an exemption from the
requirement to use a town-provided septic tank or town-managed graywater lagoon
system if they can demonstrate that an alternative system for wastewater
management meets or exceeds state and local standards and does not pose a risk
to public health or the environment.
   (B)   Approval. Exemptions shall be granted at the discretion of the Town
Council after review by the town’s designated health or environmental officer.
Approved exemptions may require periodic inspections at the property owner’s
expense.
(Ord. passed - -)
§ 50.068 FUNDING.
   The town shall establish a dedicated fund to cover the costs of septic tank
provision, installation, inspection, pumping, repairs, and replacements, as
well as the construction, inspection, and maintenance of graywater lagoon
systems. Funding may be derived from general revenues, grants, or a designated
wastewater management fee assessed to property owners, as determined by the
Town Council.
(Ord. passed - -)

30 mph on any street within the town.
   (B)   Alleyways. No vehicle shall be operated on an alley at a rate of speed
in excess of ten mph.
   (C)   Park environs. No vehicle shall be operated on the premises of any
public park at a rate of speed in excess of five mph.
   (D)   Other areas. No vehicle shall be operated at a rate of speed in excess
of 25 mph on any area not identified in this section.
   (E)   Minimum speed. No motorized vehicle shall be operated on any street
within the town at a rate of speed less than five mph. Further, no person shall
operate a vehicle upon the streets or alleys of the town in such a manner as to
slow following traffic to less than the minimum speed limit for such street or
alley, obstruct or hinder traffic, or endanger any person.
(Prior Code, § 5-2-1) Penalty, see §
70.99
§ 70.03 PARKING.
   The following provisions govern the parking of vehicles on the streets,
alleyways, and public areas within the town and the enforcement thereof.
   (A)   Alleys. No person owning or in charge of a vehicle shall park it, or
allow it to be parked, upon the traveled portion of any alley for a period
longer than 20 minutes.
   (B)   Parks. Parking is permitted in designated areas of Wingate Town Park
from one half hour before sunrise to one half hour after sunset daily, unless
otherwise posted or authorized by the town. There shall be no parking
overnight.
   (C)   Other areas. No person owning or in charge of a vehicle shall park it,
or allow it to be parked, upon a utility and emergency access way, an unpaved
portion of private property, or on a sidewalk.
   (D)   Parallel parking on streets. No person owning or in charge of a
vehicle shall park it, or allow it to be parked, in any manner other than
parallel parking. A vehicle is parallel parked if it is parked on the right-
hand side of the street as designated by the direction of lawful travel with at
least one-half the total number of its wheels being no more than 12 inches from
the curb.
   (E)   No parking signs. The Council may cause “no parking” signs to be
placed on or near any street, alley, or public way, and the same shall be
binding on all persons within the town, who shall not park a vehicle in the
area designated as “no parking” by such signs.
   (F)   Number of parking citations.
      (1)   One violation of this section occurs with respect to a vehicle
whenever such vehicle is found parked or allowed to be parked, in a prohibited
area, regardless of whether or not it has been moved since the last time so
found.
      (2)   The provisions of this section notwithstanding, no more than one
citation shall be issued with respect to an improperly parked vehicle within a
ten-minute period.
   (G)   Towing and storage of vehicles.
      (1)   The other provisions of this chapter notwithstanding, any vehicle
cited for parking in a prohibited area and found parked at any place within the
same prohibited area one hour or more after the time of the citation, whether
or not said vehicle moved during such time, may be forthwith towed to a place
of storage without notice to the owner or person in charge of the vehicle.
      (2)   In such event, the owner, and any person having charge of the
vehicle if different from the owner, shall be jointly and severally liable to
the town for the costs of towing and storage.
   (H)   Towing agent. The Town Marshal shall select, on a rotating basis,
private firms to perform towing and storage services upon vehicles as may be
requested pursuant to §
70.07.
   (I)   Return of vehicle. No vehicle towed and stored shall be returned to
any person until:
      (1)   The citation, towing, and storage charges have been paid; or
      (2)   If the citation is disputed, the owner places an amount equal to
the citation, towing, and storage charges in an escrow account from which he or
she may not withdraw funds absent consent of the Town Attorney or order of a
court of competent jurisdiction.
(Prior Code, § 5-2-2) Penalty, see §
70.99
§ 70.04 ABANDONED VEHICLES.
   This section applies to abandoned motorized vehicles found within the town.
   (A)   On public property. A motorized vehicles is ABANDONED if it appears on
public property in any one or more of the following circumstances or
conditions:
      (1)   In a manner or in a place inconsistent with the code or the law;
      (2)   Regardless of conformity to the code or the laws, without having
been moved for two consecutive days; or
      (3)   In such a manner as to constitute a hazard or obstruction to the
movement of pedestrian or vehicular traffic on a public right-of-way.
   (B)   On private property. A motorized vehicle is abandoned if it appears on
private property in any one or more of the following circumstances or
conditions:
      (1)   Without the consent of the owner or person in control of that
property for more than 24 hours; or
      (2)   Regardless of the consent of the owner or person in control of that
property, in a state of dismantlement or disrepair rendering the motorized
vehicle incapable of either operation or operation on a public highway or road
in conformity with the laws.
   (C)   On private or public property. A motorized vehicle is ABANDONED if it
appears on any public or private property as the result of towing under §
70.03 if the motorized vehicle is not claimed, bonded, or redeemed by the owner
or the owner’s agent within 20 days after the motorized vehicle’s having been
towed.
   (D)   Exception. Notwithstanding the provisions of this section, a motorized
vehicle is not abandoned on private property if it is in an enclosed building;
on the premises of a business enterprise operated in a lawful place and manner
when necessary to the operation of such enterprise; or, in a storage facility
maintained in a lawful place and manner.
   (E)   Towing and storage. The other provisions of this chapter
notwithstanding, the Town Marshal is authorized to remove or have removed any
abandoned motor vehicle and dispose of the same pursuant to I.C. 9-22-1-1 et
seq. as the same exist on the effective date of this code or as may hereafter
be amended, recodified, or reenacted.

§ 90.02 ANIMALS RUNNING AT LARGE.

   (B)   No owner or agent shall fail to exercise proper care and control of

§ 90.03 DOMESTIC PET CARE STANDARDS.
   Each owner or agent having custody or charge of, harboring, or exercising
control over a domestic pet shall provide the following minimum standards of
care for each such animal.
   (A)   Each domestic pet shall have access to a shelter which will protect it
from the weather and allow it to stand, sit, turn around, and lie down without
restriction. The shelter must be structurally sound, moisture proof and
windproof, and provide adequate protection from the cold and heat, including
bedding to provide insulation and protection against cold and dampness and
promote the retention of body heat in cold weather. The shelter must be placed
in a dry area free of debris, feces, and standing water.
   (B)   Each domestic pet shall have sufficient and wholesome food and water,
which is proper and nutritional for that species of animal.
   (C)   If a domestic pet is ill, diseased, or injured, it shall receive
proper veterinary care as necessary to promote the good health of the domestic
pet and prevent the transmittal of disease to other animals or humans.
   (D)   No domestic pet shall be abandoned, beaten, ill-treated, tormented, or
otherwise abused or neglected, or involved in any dog fight, or other fight
between animals or between animals and humans.
   (E)   A domestic pet shall be kept under restraint when in heat so as to
prevent unintentional breeding.
   (F)   If a domestic pet is chained or tethered, the chain or tether shall
not weight more than one-eighth of the animal’s body weight, shall be at least
ten feet in length and have swivels on both ends, so as to reduce the
likelihood of entanglement. A chain or tether used to restrain a domestic pet
must, by design and placement, be unlikely to become entangled.
   (G)   No domestic pet shall be kept or maintained on a tether for a period
of more than ten continuous hours, nor for no more than 12 hours in any 24-hour
period, nor for any duration under conditions which threaten the health or well
being of the domestic pet.
   (H)   A muzzle may not be worn by a dog continuously as a means for
controlling barking.
(Penalty, see § 90.99)
§ 90.04 ANIMAL WASTE.

§ 90.05 KEEPING DANGEROUS OR WILD ANIMALS.

   (B)   No person shall keep or permit to be kept on his or her premises any
wild or vicious animal for display or for exhibition purposes, whether
gratuitously or for a fee.
   (C)   This section shall not apply to zoological parks, performing animal
exhibitions, or circuses.
(Penalty, see § 90.99)
§ 90.06 OFFENSIVE SMELLS OR CONDITIONS.
   No person shall keep within the town any animal or animals in such a manner
as to create or cause any offensive or noxious smell or condition, or maintain
or use any animal pen or shelter in such manner as to permit the contents of
filth therein to run or wash upon premises owned or occupied by another or upon
any street or other public place.
(Penalty, see § 90.99)
§ 90.07 REMOVAL OF DEAD ANIMALS.
   Any person who becomes aware of the death of any animal owned by him or her
within the town shall, within six hours thereafter, dispose of the dead animal
so as not to become a nuisance. When that animal is killed by a person other
than its owner, then that person shall instead dispose of the dead animal.
§ 90.08 BARKING DOGS.
   No person shall keep within the town any animal which by loud and frequent
barking, howling, yelping or other animal noises, disturbs the peace and quiet,
or annoys any citizens.
(Penalty, see § 90.99)
§ 90.09 NAME TAGS REQUIRED.
   No owner of any domestic pet shall allow that domestic pet to be off that
owner’s premises unless the domestic pet is wearing a tag which gives the
owner’s name and address.
(Penalty, see § 90.99)
§ 90.10 RABIES.
   All dogs, cats, and ferrets three months of age or older must be vaccinated
against rabies. The rabies vaccination of a dog, cat, and ferret shall be
maintained by ongoing revaccination of the animal pursuant to State Law (345
I.A.C. § 1-5) and other guidelines set forth by the State Board of Animal
Health.
(Penalty, see § 90.99)
§ 90.11 IMPOUNDMENT AND VIOLATION NOTICE.
   (A)   Grounds for impoundment. Any law enforcement officer or animal control
agent may immediately capture and impound any of the following animals:
      (1)   Any at large animal;
      (2)   Any stray animal;
      (3)   Any wolf hybrid or coydog;
      (4)   Any unattended animal that is ill, injured, or otherwise in need of
emergency care;
      (5)   Any animal that is reasonably suspected of having rabies;
      (6)   Any unattended animal that is exhibiting aggressive or dangerous
behavior and is not sufficiently confined to the property of its owner;
      (7)   Any animal that a law enforcement officer or animal control agent
has probable cause to believe is a dangerous animal; or
      (8)   Any animal that a law enforcement officer or animal control agent
has probable cause to believe has been the subject of or involved in a
violation under I.C. 15-20-1-4 or I.C. 35-46-3.
   (B)   Actions to prevent harm to individuals or other animals. If any animal
is found at large and cannot be safely captured, a law enforcement officer may
seek assistance from an animal control agent, or take other action deemed
appropriate, including tranquilizing or killing such animal, to prevent serious
bodily injury to individuals, or severe injury to domestic pets or domestic
livestock.
   (C)   Identification of impounded animals; notice to owners.
      (1)   Any animals which are impounded pursuant to this chapter shall be
scanned or examined by the animal control facility for a microchip, collar tag,
or other identification containing the owner’s name, address, and/or phone
number, unless doing so presents an unreasonable risk of serious bodily injury
to an animal control agent.
      (2)   (a)   If an impounded animal’s owner can be identified, the animal
control facility shall notify the owner by the end of the next business day
that the animal has been impounded, and that unless the animal is claimed by
the owner within five days from the date of impoundment, the animal may be
placed for adoption or humanely euthanized.
         (b)   Notwithstanding the foregoing, in the case of a second or
subsequent impoundment, the impounded animal may be placed for adoption or
humanely euthanized if not claimed within three days after the owner is
notified.
      (3)   Stray animals without any means of identification of their owners
shall be held at the animal control facility a minimum of three days in order
to permit an owner adequate time to reclaim them. A stray animal which is
unclaimed after having been impounded for three days may be placed for adoption
or humanely euthanized, except that the stray shall be euthanized if it is a
dangerous animal.
   (D)   Release from impoundment.
      (1)   A non-dangerous animal may be returned to its owner if the
following conditions are met.
         (a)   Costs of impoundment. The owner of an impounded animal shall be
responsible for all costs of impoundment, and must pay such costs in full prior
to the animal’s release from impoundment.
         (b)   Microchip implantation. Prior to the return to its owner of any
impounded dog or cat which at the time of impoundment did not bear a microchip,
collar tag or other means of identification, the animal control agency shall
cause a microchip with a registered identification number to be implanted in
the dog or cat at the owner’s expense. The animal control agency shall be
entitled to retain the dog or cat until the microchip implantation fee is paid.
         (c)   Payment of county option dog tax. No dog impounded pursuant to
this chapter shall be released to its owner until the owner presents proof of
payment of all applicable county option dog taxes with respect to such dog. Any
dog so impounded shall be considered unclaimed until such tax is paid.
         (d)   Spay or neuter. No dog or cat impounded pursuant to this chapter
shall be released to its owner until the owner either presents proof that the
animal is spayed or neutered, or otherwise altered to prevent it from
procreating, or makes representation that the animal shall be spayed/neutered
within a time period specified by the animal control officer, and provides
proof thereafter.
         (e)   Proof of rabies vaccination. No dog, cat, or ferret three months
of age or older impounded pursuant to this chapter shall be released to its
owner until the owner presents proof of current rabies vaccination.
         (f)   Payment of fines. No animal impounded pursuant to this chapter
shall be released to its owner until the owner pays all fines and costs imposed
by this chapter.
      (2)   The return of a dangerous animal to its owner is governed by §
90.12.
   (E)   Treatment or euthanizing of sick or injured animals. The animal
control facility shall have authority to take whatever action is reasonably
necessary, including humane euthanization, to deal with a sick or injured
animal, to prevent unnecessary suffering of the animal, or to prevent the
spread of communicable diseases. Nothing in this chapter shall limit the animal
control facility’s ability to take whatever action is reasonably necessary to
provide veterinary care by a veterinarian for a sick or injured animal.
(Penalty, see § 90.99)
§ 90.12 IMPOUNDMENT OF DANGEROUS ANIMALS.
   (A)   Impoundment. A dangerous animal which has been captured and impounded
by a law enforcement officer or animal control agent shall remain impounded
subject to the requirements of this section.
   (B)   Euthanization of dangerous animals. A dangerous animal which has been
impounded shall be euthanized if:
      (1)   The owner fails to request a hearing before the Town Council
pursuant to division (C) below within five days after having been notified of
the impoundment;
      (2)   The owner waives in writing all ownership interest in the dangerous
animal; or
      (3)   The owner cannot be identified or located, and the animal remains
unclaimed for three days after having been impounded.
   (C)   Hearings and appeals. If an owner makes a timely request for a hearing
pursuant to division (B)(1) above, the Town Council shall conduct a public
hearing.
      (1)   At such hearing, a law enforcement officer, animal control officer,
or animal control agency shall present evidence supporting a determination that
the animal is a dangerous animal, and the owner shall have the opportunity to
confront and cross-examine the witnesses supporting such determination, and to
present evidence opposing the determination.
      (2)   Following the hearing, the Town Council shall make a finding by a
preponderance of the evidence presented at the hearing whether the animal is a
dangerous animal.
      (3)   If the Town Council determines that the animal is not a dangerous
animal, it shall order the animal released to the owner, subject to the
requirements of §
90.11(D)(1).
      (4)   If the Town Council determines that the animal is a dangerous
animal, the animal shall be retained by an animal control agency and must be
permanently removed from the corporate limits of the town within three days
after said determination, with the costs of keeping and removing the animal all
to be at the owner’s expense.
(Penalty, see § 90.99)
§ 90.13 BREACH OF FACILITY; INTERFERENCE WITH AGENT.
   (A)   No person other than an authorized employee or agent of an animal
control facility shall release any animal impounded therein.
   (B)   No person shall interfere with the taking of an animal by an animal
control agent.
(Penalty, see § 90.99)
§ 90.14 PROOF OF VIOLATION AND ENFORCEMENT.
   The demonstration of a prima facie violation under this chapter may be shown
by any combination of the following evidence:
   (A)   Observation or other confirmation by a law enforcement officer, animal
control agent, or other duly-authorized municipal code enforcement agent;
   (B)   A written statement from a complaining witness with personal knowledge
of the alleged violation at issue;
   (C)   An audio recording by a complaining witness with personal knowledge of
the alleged violation at issue; or
   (D)   A video recording or photograph by a complaining witness with personal
knowledge of the alleged violation at issue.
(Penalty, see § 90.99)
§ 90.15 NUISANCES; ANIMAL PERMIT FOR 4-H AND FFA MEMBERS.
   (A)   Definitions.
      4-H. A youth organization administered by the National Institute of Food
and Agriculture of the United States Department of Agriculture and the name
represents four personal development areas of focus for the organization: head,
heart, hands, and health.
      FFA. The Future Farmers of America.
      YOUTH. A person 19 years old or younger.
   (B)   Animal permit for 4-H and FFA members.
      (1)   A youth who is a member of 4-H or FFA may obtain an initial permit
to raise up to 20 total poultry, rabbits, and or waterfowl, in any zoning
district in the town for any 4-H or FFA project.
      (2)   Applications for an animal permit shall be obtained from the Town
Clerk-Treasurer and shall be effective for one full calendar year.
      (3)   There shall be no fee charged by the town for this permit.
      (4)   All applications for an animal permit must be filed with the Town
Clerk-Treasurer by and shall be approved by the Town Council, within a
reasonable amount of time after review and consideration.
      (5)   The permit may be revoked by the Town Council for cause shown.
   (C)   Membership.
      (1)   All applicants must be able to prove membership either in 4-H or
FFA.
      (2)   After a permit has been issued, the applicant is under a continuing
duty to prove membership either in 4-H or FFA if required by the town.
   (D)   Requirements for permit.
      (1)   Applicants must contact their neighbors before turning in an
application to advise the neighbors about the type and number of animals they
want to raise and the location where the animals will be kept.
      (2)   The youth must have a land area adequate enough for the animals,
according to the Board of Animal Health regulations.
      (3)   All animals shall be maintained in a healthy condition, or if ill,
shall be given appropriate treatment.
      (4)   The structure in which animals are kept shall be maintained in a
clean condition and a good state of repair. The structure shall allow adequate
protection against weather extremes.
      (5)   Structures housing animals shall be sufficiently large to permit
freedom of movement for the animals.
      (6)   If a youth is keeping a rooster and it creates noise which bother
neighbors, the rooster shall be removed from the premises.
      (8)   Food supplies shall be of sufficient quantity and nutritional value
to meet the daily requirements of the animals.
      (9)   All animals must be terminal.
      (10)   Applicants must provide the town with a written plan for animal
waste disposal.
      (11)   All structures and holding areas for the animals must be set back
at least ten feet from any property line. However, this can be modified by the
Town Council upon written request of the applicant.
   (E)   Right of entry. The Town Marshal and/or any member of the Town Council
shall have the right to enter upon private property where the animals are kept
during reasonable daytime hours to inspect the premises to determine compliance
with this ordinance. If entry is refused, the Town Marshal can obtain an
inspection warrant as authorized under State Code.
(Ord. 2022-01, passed 3-14-2023)
§ 90.99 PENALTY.
   (A)   Violations. Any person who is in violation of this chapter shall be
deemed to have committed a civil ordinance violation and may be issued a
citation by the designated enforcement entity.
      (1)   (a)   Each day a violation remains uncorrected is a distinct and
separate ordinance violation subject to an additional citation and fine in the
amount prescribed below.
         (b)   However, two or more violations that are linked closely in time
and/or nature may, in the sole discretion of the enforcement entity, be deemed
one violation.
      (2)   The monetary fine for each civil animal control violation shall be
$25, except that for a repeated animal control violation by the same person,
the following fines shall apply:
         (a)   Second citation: $50; and
         (b)   Each citation in excess of two: $100.
      (3)   Further, in addition to all other penalties provided by law, any
person who shall violate this animal control chapter five or more separate
times in one calendar year period shall be fined an additional $500 as an
habitual animal control violator.
      (4)   All fines for animal control violations shall be paid within five
days to the Town Clerk-Treasurer, who shall render to the person making the
payment a receipt stating the amount and purpose for which the fine has been
paid. All fines thus received shall be deposited with the Town Clerk-Treasurer.
   (B)   Enforcement.
      (1)   Law enforcement officers and animal control officers may issue
notices of civil animal control violation(s) to a person who commits a civil
animal control violation, by doing so either to the owner or the owner’s agent.
The citation may be served by personal service, by certified mail, or by a
placement in a conspicuous place on the property where the violation occurs and
shall serve as notice to a person that the person has committed an animal
control ordinance violation.
      (2)   If the violation is determined by the law enforcement officer or
animal control officer to be a threat to public health or safety, that officer
may take any and all further enforcement and/or abatement measures allowed by
law.
   (C)   Appeal. Any owner wishing to appeal or clarify any citation or
requirement of this chapter may make an appeal to the Town Council within five
days of receipt of that citation.
   (D)   Trial for animal control violations.
      (1)   A person who receives a ticket or citation may elect to stand trial
for the offense by indicating on the ticket or citation that person’s intent to
stand trial and returning a copy of the citation to the Clerk-Treasurer. The
returned copy of the ticket or citation shall serve as notice of the person’s
intent to stand trial. On receipt of the notice of intention to stand trial, a
lawsuit will be commenced by the Town Attorney in a court of competent
jurisdiction in the county and the matter shall proceed as any other ordinance
violation proceeding.
      (2)   If a person who receives a citation fails to pay the assessed fine
within five days or fails to give notice of that person’s intention to stand
trial as prescribed above, the Town Attorney may file a civil lawsuit as
prescribed by applicable laws and ordinances, and seek penalties as prescribed
in this chapter.
      (3)   In addition to any fines above, a person adjudged to have committed
a civil animal control violation is liable and responsible for all fees, costs
and expenses involved in abatement, enforcement, and prosecution of that
violation, including, but not limited to, impoundment and boarding expenses,
veterinary and other animal-care expenses, clean-up costs, reasonable attorney
fees, costs of giving notices, and court costs. Each day a violation continues
shall constitute a separate offense.

**** OPEN BURNING ****
§ 91.01 DEFINITION.
   For the purpose of this subchapter, the following definition applies unless
the context clearly indicates or requires a different meaning.
   OPEN BURNING. The burning of any materials wherein air contaminants
resulting from combustion are emitted directly into the air, without passing
through a stack or chimney from an enclosed chamber.
(Ord. 2023 02, passed 8-8-2023)
§ 91.02 PROHIBITION.
   No person shall start, kindle, cause, allow, or maintain any form of open
burning of any materials on private or public property, except as specifically
authorized by this subchapter. No person shall allow the accumulation or
existence of combustible material that constitutes or contributes to open
burning.
(Ord. 2023 02, passed 8-8-2023) Penalty, see §
91.99
§ 91.03 EXEMPTIONS, VARIANCES, AND CONDITIONS.
   (A)   Exemptions. 
      (1)   The following types of burning are allowed: the burning of
charcoal, clean untreated wood, and other cooking fuels customarily used in an
outdoor grill, traditional food cooking devices, or campfires.
      (2)   RECREATIONAL FIRES shall be defined as an outdoor fire whose
purpose is for pleasure, religious, ceremonial, cooking, warmth, or similar
purposes.
      (3)   Only clean, untreated wood or charcoal shall be used. Paper or
petroleum products can be used for ignition purposes only. Construction
materials or yard waste are strictly prohibited.
      (4)   The fire shall not be ignited more than two hours before the
recreational activity is to take place and shall be extinguished upon the
conclusion of the activity.
      (5)   The pile to be burned shall be less than 1,000 cubic feet (for
example: ten feet by ten feet by ten feet).
      (6)   The local Fire Department shall be notified 24 hours in advance if
the pile to be burned is more than 125 cubic feet (for example: five feet by
five feet by five feet).
      (7)   The fire shall not be for disposal purposes.
      (8)   The fire shall not be within 500 feet of a pipeline or fuel storage
area.
   (B)   Variances.
      (1)   Other types of fires may be approved as follows: Any other type of
fire whereby a citizen of the town has obtained a variance from the provisions
of this subchapter by petitioning the Town Council may be allowed. However, the
Town Council cannot grant a variance for burning that would otherwise violate
the provisions of the 326 I.A.C. 4-1 et seq. and as amended and I.C. 13-17-9.
      (2)   The following types of fires may be allowed if approved by the
State Department of Environmental Management:
         (a)   Fire fighter training;
         (b)   Fire extinguisher training;
         (c)   Vegetation propagation;
         (d)   Use of an air curtain destructor; and
         (e)   Please refer to Attachment A of the ordinance from which this
section is derived for expanded version of variance situations based on 326
I.A.C. 4-1-3(c)(3-8) and 326 I.A.C. 4-1-4.
   (C)   Conditions. The following conditions apply to all exemptions and
variances.
      (1)   Burning shall be done during safe weather conditions. Burning shall
not occur during high winds, temperature inversions, air stagnation, or when a
pollution alert or ozone action day has been declared.
      (2)   Fires must be attended at all times until completely extinguished.
      (3)   Fires must be extinguished if they create a fire hazard nuisance,
based on complaints of breathing of others, pollution problem, or threat to
public health.
      (4)   Fire fighting equipment adequate for the size of the fire shall be
on-site and nearby during times of burning.
      (5)   Burning shall not be for disposal purposes.
      (6)   All burning shall comply with other federal, state, and local laws,
rules, and ordinances.
(Ord. 2023 02, passed 8-8-2023) Penalty, see §
91.99
§ 91.04 ENFORCEMENT.
   (A)   Any person found in violation of this subchapter shall be subject to
the following procedures.
      (1)   The Fire/Police/Health Department/Solid Waste District/or other
designated department personnel shall issue a warning notice to a first-time
violator stating that he or she is in violation. The person must then correct
the violation by immediately extinguishing the fire. Failure or refusal to
immediately extinguish the fire shall result in a citation being issued.
      (2)   Upon issuance of a citation, the violator shall be fined in
accordance with §
91.99(B).
      (3)   Violators may also be assessed reasonable attorney fees and costs
of collection incurred in enforcement of this subchapter.
      (4)   Failure or refusal by the violator to immediately extinguish the
fire in violation of this subchapter shall also result in the Fire Department
having the authority to go upon private property to extinguish said fire.
      (5)   Each subsequent starting, kindling, causing, or allowing of a new
fire after a warning notice or citation has been issued, shall be considered a
separate offense.
      (6)   Any person who allows the accumulation or existence of combustible
material which constitutes or contributes to open burning may not refute
liability for violation of this subchapter on the basis that said fire was set
by vandals, accidental, or act of God.
   (B)   The open burning provisions are enforceable by the duly appointed
Fire/Police/Health Department/Solid Waste District/or other designated
department personnel or law enforcement officers within the town, acting on his
or her own initiative or at the request of the Town Council.
(Ord. 2023 02, passed 8-8-2023)
§ 91.05 BURNING RESTRICTIONS AND REGULATIONS.
   (A)   Waste oil burning where waste oil originates from spillage during
testing of an oil well and has been collected in a properly constructed and
located burn-off pit as prescribed in 310 I.A.C. § 7-l-37(a) in the Indiana
Department of Natural Resources (DNR) rules, oil, and gas operations. Burning
shall be subject to the following conditions:
      (1)   Each oil pit may be burned once every two months; and
      (2)   The fire must be extinguished within 30 minutes of ignition.
   (B)   Indiana Department of Natural Resources (DNR) burning, to facilitate
prescribed burning on DNR -controlled properties for wildlife habitat
maintenance, forestry purposes, natural area management, and fire fighting or
prevention; United States Department of the Interior burning, to facilitate a
National Park Service Fire Management Plan for the Indiana Dunes National
Lakeshore, for example; and United States Department of Agriculture, Forest
Service burning, to facilitate wildlife habitat maintenance, forestry purposes,
natural area management, ecosystem management, and firefighting or prevention.
   (C)   Burning by the United States Forest Service for firefighting or
prevention is not subject to the conditions in §
91.03(C).
   (D)   Burning of marijuana by federal, state, and local law enforcement
offices. Only clean petroleum products shall be used for ignition purposes.
   (E)   Burning, for the purpose of heating, shall be performed using clean
wood products or paper in a noncombustible container that is sufficiently
vented to induce adequate primary combustion, and has enclosed sides and a
bottom. Burning shall be subject to the following conditions:
      (1)   Burning shall only occur between October 1 and May 15; and
      (2)   Burning shall not be conducted for the purpose of disposal.
   (F)   Burning of vegetation by fire departments and firefighters to create
fire breaks for purposes of extinguishing an existing fire. Such burning is not
subject to the conditions in §
91.03(C).
   (G)   Burning of clean petroleum products for fire extinguisher training,
subject the following conditions.
      (1)   The Local Fire Department and Health Department must be notified at
least 24 hours in advance of the date, time, and location of the burning.
      (2)   All burning shall take place in a noncombustible container or
enclosure, enclosed on all sides with a bottom.
      (3)   A total of no more than 14 gallons of fuel may be burned per day.
      (4)   Only one fire may be allowed to burn at a time.
      (5)   All burning shall be conducted in such a manner so as to prevent
any possibility of soil contamination.
   (H)   Emergency burning with prior oral approval of the Commissioner of the
State Department of Environmental Management or the Commissioner’s designated
agent may be authorized for the following:
      (1)   Spilled or escaping liquid or gaseous petroleum products when all
reasonable efforts to recover the spilled material have been made and failure
to burn would result in an imminent fire or health hazard or air or water
pollution problem; or
      (2)   Clean wood waste, vegetation, or deceased animals resulting from a
natural disaster where failure to burn would result in an imminent health or
safety hazard.
(Ord. 2023 02, passed 8-8-2023)
§ 91.99 PENALTY.
   (A)   Any person violating any provision of this chapter for which no
specific penalty is prescribed shall be subject to §
10.99 of this code of ordinances.
   (B)   (1)   Upon issuance of a citation, as stated in §
91.04, the violator shall be fined as follows:
         (a)   First offense: $50;
         (b)   Second offense: $75; and
         (c)   Third offense: $150.
      (2)   A separate offense shall be deemed committed on each day during
which a violation occurs or continues.
(Ord. 2023 02, passed 8-8-2023)

**** PUBLIC NUISANCES ****
§ 92.01 DECLARATION.
   Each of the following conditions are hereby declared a public nuisance and a
danger to the health, safety, and welfare of the people of the town:
   (A)   The existence of weeds, trees, shrubs, plants, vines, or other
vegetation, whether the same be living, dead, decayed, broken, or dying, which
is:
      (1)   Of such a character or to such an extent as to interfere with
health, safety, or public welfare; or
      (2)   Of such a character or to such an extent as to interfere with the
safe and lawful use of any real estate, easement, or right-of-way by any person
or the town.
   (B)   The existence of any tree or vegetation, whether the same be living,
dead, decayed, broken, or dying, which overhangs the surface of a public way
below a clear height of 13 feet six inches, or which interferes with the
passage of light from a street lighting system;
   (C)   The existence of any waste, garbage, or trash upon property, whether
or not the same be in boxes, bags, or any other containers, for a period longer
than seven consecutive days, or of such a character or to such an extent as to:
      (1)   Interfere with health, safety, or public welfare;
      (2)   Interfere with the safe and lawful use of any real estate,
easement, or right-of-way by an person;
      (3)   Exude unpleasant odors; or
      (4)   Attract animals.
   (D)   The continuous or periodic presence of rats, mice, insects, parasites,
or vermin, or the existence of an environment favorable to their habitation
and/or multiplication, on three consecutive days, or of such a character or to
such an extent as to:
      (1)   Interfere with health, safety, or public welfare;
      (2)   Interfere with the safe and lawful use of any real estate,
easement, or right-of-way by any person; or
      (3)   Exude unpleasant odors.
   (E)   The existence of any personal property not in an enclosed building on

   (A)   Upon determining that a public nuisance exists, the Town Marshal shall

   (B)   Any person receiving notice of a violation of this subchapter shall,

   (C)   If, after 15 days following service of the notice, such nuisance is

this subchapter.

to determine the identity and address of the person to whom notice is required

by posting such notice in a prominent place on the property where the nuisance
was found.
   (E)   If the abating, removing, or curing of a nuisance by the town under
division (C) above results in the possession of any saleable personal property,
the same may be sold by the town at such times and places, and be for such
price and terms as the Council deems convenient, and the proceeds thereof
forfeited and made the property of the town to defray costs.

at the request of the town in the investigation, abatement, curing, or removal
of any nuisance.

§ 92.21 DEFINITIONS AND INTERPRETATIONS.

For the purpose of this subchapter, the following definitions apply unless the

   PERSON. Any individual, partnership, limited partnership, limited liability
partnership, limited liability company, association, corporation, or other
entity.
   PREMISES. A tract of land, including its buildings and structures.
   SOUND AMPLIFICATION SYSTEM. Any apparatus used for the amplification of

including any apparatus for the amplification of the human voice.
   TOWN. The Town of Wingate, Indiana.
   TOWN COUNCIL. The Town Council of the Town of Wingate, Indiana.
   UNREASONABLE OR EXCESSIVE NOISE. Sound that is of a volume, frequency, or
pattern that prevents, disrupts, injures, or endangers the health, safety,
welfare, prosperity, comfort, or repose of reasonable persons of ordinary
sensitivities within the corporate boundaries of the town, given the time of

   VEHICLE. A device in, upon, or by which a person or property is, or may be,

§ 92.22 PROHIBITED NOISES.
   (A)   Unreasonable or excessive noise. Except as otherwise provided in this
subchapter, it shall be a violation of this subchapter for any person to cause
or make any unreasonable or excessive noise or to allow any unreasonable or
excessive noise to be caused or made in or on any real property or personal
property within the town limits occupied or controlled by that person.
UNREASONABLE OR EXCESSIVE NOISE shall also be defined as follows.
      (1)   Noise emanating from any public property, including any public
right-of-way, highway, building, sidewalk, public space, park, or thoroughfare,
and the sound generated therefrom is:
         (a)   Audible 40 feet or more from its source; or
         (b)   Is it a level of 90 decibels (90dB) or more when measured on a
dB(A) scale from a distance of not less than six feet from its source.
      (2)   Noise emanating from any private property and the sound generated
therefrom is:
         (a)   Audible 40 feet or more outside of said private property line;
or
         (b)   Is at a level of 90 decibels (90dB) or more when measured on a
dB(A) scale from a distance of not less than six feet from said private
property line.
   (B)   Specific prohibitions. In addition to the general noise prohibition in
division (A) above, the following acts within the town limits are violations of
this subchapter.
      (1)   Motor vehicle noises.
         (a)   No person shall operate any motor vehicle on a public street,
road, alley, highway, park, or other public property in which the engine,
muffler, exhaust, and/or other noise control equipment have been altered,
removed, or maintained in such disrepair as to generate an unreasonable or
excessive noise.
         (b)   No person shall operate or use any motor vehicle in such a
manner to create unreasonable or excessive noise from squealing of tires or
grating, grinding, rattling, or other noises from disrepair.
         (c)   No person operating a motor vehicle containing an engine brake
system shall apply the engine brakes.
         (d)   Except as used for warning purposes, no person shall use,
operate, or sound any horn or other signaling device on any motor vehicle on a
public street, road, alley, highway, park, or other public property for any
unreasonable period of time.
         (e)   No person shall play, use, or operate, or allow to be played,
used, or operated, in or on any motor vehicle any sound amplification system at
a volume that is louder than necessary for the convenient hearing of persons
who are inside the motor vehicle within which such device is played, used, or
operated.
      (2)   Noises from premises.
         (a)   No person shall play, use, or operate or allow to be played,
used, or operated on a premises any sound amplification system in such a manner
that the sound produced causes unreasonable or excessive noise heard beyond the
boundaries of the premises by a person with normal hearing.
         (b)   No person shall keep any animal, including, but not limited to,
dogs and birds, on a premises that causes unreasonable or excessive noise
clearly audible to a person from any place other than the premises from which
the animal is located.
      (3)   Machine noises.
         (a)   No person shall engage in the erection, excavation, demolition,
alteration, or repair of any building or real estate causing unreasonable or
excessive noise between the hours of 9:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m.
         (b)   No person shall engage in the operation, alteration, or repair
of any pile driver, hydraulic shovel or grader, pneumatic hammer, hoist,
blower, fan, or other equipment causing unreasonable or excessive noise between
the hours of 9:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m.
(Ord. 2020-02, passed 11-13-2020) Penalty, see §
92.99

   (L)   Any other noise resulting from activities of a temporary duration
permitted by the Town Council in accordance with §
92.24.

ABANDONED AND JUNK VEHICLES
§ 92.40 PURPOSE.
   The purpose of this subchapter shall be to implement the procedure contained
in I.C. 9-22-1 which govern the removal, storage, and disposal of abandoned
vehicles.
§ 92.41 DEFINITIONS.
   For the purpose of this subchapter, the following definitions shall apply
unless the context clearly indicates or requires a different meaning.
   ABANDONED VEHICLE.
      (1)   A vehicle located on public property illegally;
      (2)   A vehicle left on public property without being moved for 24 hours;
      (3)   A vehicle located on public property in such a manner as to
constitute a hazard or obstruction to the movement of pedestrian or vehicular
traffic on a public right-of-way;
      (4)   A vehicle that has remained on private property without the consent
of the owner or person in control of that property for more than 48 hours;
      (5)   A vehicle from which the engine, transmission or differential has
been removed, or that
is otherwise partially dismantled or inoperable and left on public property; or
      (6)   A vehicle that is at least three model-years old, is mechanically
inoperable, and remains on private property, continuously, in a location or
locations visible from public property for more than 20 days. For purposes of
this paragraph, a vehicle covered by a tarpaulin or other plastic, vinyl,
rubber, cloth, or textile covering is considered to be visible.
   JUNK.
      (1)   Any articles in any form composed of or consisting of any of the
following:
         (a)   Secondhand, discarded, abandoned, or castoff building materials,
including without limitation, lumber, cabinets, doors, windows, siding, metals
or materials, namely iron, brass, bronze, copper, tin, zinc, lead, or any other
metals or compounds thereof;
         (b)   Broken glass, rags, clothing, rubber, plastics and synthetic
substances and fabrics, bottles, papers, feathers, any other waste material, or
any compound or byproduct of any of the foregoing enumerated materials;
         (c)   Furnaces, central air units, refrigerators, washing machines,
televisions, computer monitors, mattresses, sofas, furniture and doors not in
current use; and
         (d)   Wrecked, abandoned or dismantled automobiles or parts thereof.
      (2)   As used in this subchapter, a JUNK CAR shall include any motor
vehicle, as defined by I.C. 9-13-2-196, including any vehicle that does not
bear a currently valid license plate and is not kept in a garage or building. A
classic motor vehicle that is under active restoration would not be considered
to be a JUNK CAR.
   LITTER. Includes, but is not limited to, garbage, trash, debris, lawn or
garden waste, newspapers, magazines, glass, metal, plastic or paper containers;
construction materials (except for those being used as part of an ongoing and
active building or repair project permitted under other applicable town
ordinances) and construction debris; scrap metal; discarded clothing; household
appliances; furniture (except for customary outdoor patio furniture in good and
usable condition); discarded or unused recreational equipment and toys; motor
vehicle, trailer and boat parts; or other nauseous or offensive matter of any
kind, that is not kept in a designated container designed to prevent its escape
and picked up (emptied) on a routinely scheduled basis. LITTER kept in or on a
trailer or truck bed shall not be deemed to be kept in a designated container.
   OPEN VIEW. Visible from private property and/or public property including,
without limitation, public roads and rights-of-way.
   UNREGISTERED VEHICLE. All vehicles except for those excepted in and pursuant
to I.C. 9-18.1-2-2, 9-18.1-2-4 through 9-18.1-2-9. A vehicle required to be
registered, pursuant to I.C. 9-18.1-2-3, shall be deemed to be an UNREGISTERED
VEHICLE unless a valid license plate, issued uniquely for that vehicle, is
securely fastened to it in the manner prescribed by I.C. 9-18.1-4-4, which, for
passenger vehicles, pick-up trucks and motorcycles, shall be upon the rear of
the vehicle, as follows:
      (1)   A license plate shall be:
         (a)   Securely fastened, in a horizontal and upright position that
displays the registration expiration year in the upper right comer, to the
vehicle for which the plate is issued:
            1.   To prevent the license plate from swinging;
            2.   At a height of at least 12 inches from the ground, measuring
from the bottom of the license plate; and
            3.   In a place and position that are clearly visible.
         (b)   Maintained free from foreign materials and in a condition to be
clearly legible; and
         (c)   Not obstructed or obscured by tires, bumpers, accessories, or
other opaque objects.
      (2)   An interim license plate issued or used by a dealer licensed under
I.C. 9-32 or used by a manufacturer must be displayed:
         (a)   In the manner required under division (1) above for the type of
vehicle on which the interim license plate is displayed; or
         (b)   In a location on the left side of a window that is:
            1.   Facing the rear of the motor vehicle; and
            2.   Clearly visible and unobstructed. A plate displayed under
division (2) above must be affixed to the window of the motor vehicle.
      (3)   Upon the renewal of a registration under this article, a license
plate other than a temporary license plate must display a renewal sticker:
         (a)   That is securely affixed in the upper right corner of the
license plate; and
         (b)   That covers the previous registration expiration year.
   VEHICLE. A device in, upon, or by which a person or property is, or may be,
transported or drawn upon a highway. The term includes automobiles, cars,
trucks, motorcycles, trailers, off-road vehicles (as defined in I.C. 14-8-2-
185), manufactured and mobile homes, and watercraft (as defined in I.C. 9-13-2-
198.5). The term does not include a device moved by human power, a wheelchair,
or an electric personal assistive mobility device.

enclosed buildings.
(Penalty, see § 92.99)

remain in the open upon public or private property within the town. Junk and/or

property, or within 15 days from private property, after such notice, the Town
Marshal, or his or her designee, shall issue a written order of violation of
the section, and the violating party shall be subject to civil penalties.

(Penalty, see § 92.99)

cited above if it is public property, or within 15 days after notice cited
above if it is private property, the Town Marshal, or his or her designee, may
then enter onto the property and remove the materials. If anyone with an
interest in the property objects to the entry of the Town Marshal in the
enforcement of this subchapter, the town may proceed to court for a court order
authorizing entry.
§ 92.46 CHARGE FOR REMOVAL DECLARED A LIEN.
   (A)   In the event the removal of items by the town is required as a
consequence of violations of this subchapter after notice, the reasonable
charge for the removal shall be levied upon and is hereby declared a lien
against the property from which the same was removed. The lien shall be
certified by the Town Clerk-Treasurer to the County Auditor. The charge shall
become a lien and attach to the real estate from the time of the filing.
   (B)   Upon the filing of any lien, the owners of any property involved will
be notified by sending a copy of the lien by the United States Postal Service
to the postal address of the property involved. Anyone with an interest in the
property who objects to the filing of the lien may appeal the filing to the
Town Council within 30 days of the date of the sending of the notice.
§ 92.99 PENALTY.
   (A)   Any person violating any provision of this chapter for which no
specific penalty is prescribed shall be subject to §
10.99 of this code of ordinances.
   (B)   (1)   The Town Council and the Town Marshal, or their designee, may at
any time require the owner and/or occupant of any property upon which a
nuisance as herein defined exists to do all things necessary to remove the
nuisance from such property by giving the owner and/or occupant 15 days’
written notice to the existence of the nuisance. The notice as herein required
shall state the nature of the alleged nuisance and the action deemed necessary
to correct the condition, and shall fix a date not sooner than 15 days from the
date of the receipt of the notice when said property owner and/or occupant may
appear before the Town Council to be heard on the question of the nuisance.
      (2)   All notices as herein required shall be sent by first-class U.S.
mail, postage prepaid, to the occupant or owner at the address of the owner as
reflected in the tax rolls of the town, township, or the county.
      (3)   Upon the failure of the owner and/or occupant to cause the
abatement of a nuisance as required by this section and after notice and
opportunity for hearing before the Town Council, the Town Council, upon a
finding that a nuisance exists and that it has not been abated, may proceed at
once to cause to be abated the nuisance, shall charge the costs thereof against

§ 130.01 DEFINITIONS.
   For the purpose of this chapter, the following definitions apply unless the
context clearly indicates or requires a different meaning.
   AMMUNITION. Any thing designed or redesigned, manufactured or
remanufactured, modified, made or able to be thrown, propelled, or shot by a
firearm. Unless otherwise excepted, the term includes any commonly made, sold,
or used component which must be assembled with other items in order to function
as AMMUNITION.
   CONSUMER FIREWORK. A small firework that is designed primarily to produce
visible or audible effects by combustion, and that is required to comply with
the construction, chemical composition, and labeling regulations promulgated by
the United States Consumer Product Safety Commission under 16 C.F.R. § 1507.
The term also includes some small devices designed to produce an audible
effect, such as whistling devices, ground devices containing 50 milligrams or
less of explosive composition, and aerial devices containing 130 milligrams or
less of explosive composition. Propelling and expelling charges consisting of a
mixture of charcoal, sulfur, and potassium nitrate are not considered to be
designed to produce an audible effect.
      (1)   CONSUMER FIREWORKS include:
         (a)   Aerial devices, including, but are not limited to, sky rockets,
missile-type rockets, helicopter and aerial spinners, Roman candles, mines, and
shells;
         (b)   Ground audible devices, including, but are not limited to,
firecrackers, salutes, and chasers; and
         (c)   Firework devices containing combinations of effects described in
divisions (1)(a) and (1)(b) above.
      (2)   CONSUMER FIREWORKS do not include the following fireworks as
referenced in I.C. 22-11-14-8(a):
         (a)   Dipped sticks or wire sparklers. However, the total pyrotechnic
composition may not exceed 100 grams per item; and, devices containing chlorate
or per chlorate salts may not exceed five grams per item;
         (b)   Cylindrical fountains;
         (c)   Cone fountains;
         (d)   Illuminating torches;
         (e)   Wheels;
         (f)   Ground spinners;
         (g)   Flitter sparklers;
         (h)   Snakes or glow worms;
         (i)   Smoke devices; and
         (j)   Trick noisemakers that include party poppers, booby traps,
snappers, trick matches, cigarette loads, and auto burglar alarms.
   DISCHARGE. Propelling, throwing, or shooting any thing by means of a
firearm.
   FIREARM. Any weapon that is capable of expelling, designed to expel or may
readily be converted to expel a projectile by means of an explosion. The term
FIREARM shall include handguns, machine guns, rifles, shotguns, and all other
weapons which fit the definition of FIREARM as provided herein, as defined by
I.C. 35-47-1 et seq., or any other applicable state or federal laws.
   FIREWORK. Any composition or device designed for the purpose of producing a
visible or audible effect by combustion, deflagration, or detonation. FIREWORKS
consist of consumer fireworks, items referenced in I.C. 22-11-14-8(a), and
special fireworks. The following items are excluded from the definition of
FIREWORKS:
      (1)   Model rockets;
      (2)   Toy pistol caps;
      (3)   Emergency signal flares;
      (4)   Matches;
      (5)   Fixed ammunition for firearms;
      (6)   Ammunition components intended for use in firearms, muzzle-loading
cannons, or small arms;
      (7)   Shells, cartridges, and primers for use in firearms, muzzle-loading
cannons, or small arms;
      (8)   Indoor, pyrotechnics, special effects material; and
      (9)   M-80s, cherry bombs, silver salutes, and any device banned by the
federal government.
   PARADE. Any continuous movement of three or more vehicles or persons through
the streets, alleys, or sidewalks of the town in such a manner as to
communicate, celebrate, publicize, or display any message, idea, cause, person,
or day.
   PERSON. An individual, association, organization, limited liability company,
or corporation.
   WEAPON. Knife with a blade longer than one inch and any device or item
which, when used, is readily capable of causing serious bodily injury as
defined by the laws of the state.
(Prior Code, § 7-1-1)

   (D)   Additional restrictions. During those exceptional times, when consumer
fireworks may be used, ignited, and discharged within the corporate limits of
the town, as prescribed in division (C) above, the following restrictions
apply:
      (1)   No person may ignite, discharge, or use consumer fireworks at a

         (a)   A special discharge location;
         (b)   The property of the person; or
         (c)   The property of another who has given permission to use the
consumer fireworks.
      (2)   No person less than 18 years of age may possess or use a firework
unless an adult is present and responsible at the location of the possession.
   (D)   Enforcement. The Town Marshal and Police Officers shall be charged
with enforcing the provisions of this section.
   (E)   Comparative state law.
      (1)   State law states that consumer fireworks may be used only between
9:00 a.m. and 11:00 p.m. on most other days that are not holidays.
      (2)   The town’s laws are more restrictive (5:00 p.m. to 11:00 p.m.) than
state law on the non-holiday time frame.
      (3)   The town cannot be less strict than the 9:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m.
regulation on holidays. Anyone who violates the majority of the state law
commits a Class A misdemeanor. Shooting fireworks before 9:00 a.m. or after 11:
00 p.m. (except as provided on July 4, December 31, and January 1) is a Class C
infraction.
      (4)   Anyone who sets off fireworks in a place other than their property,
the property of someone who has given them permission, or a “special discharge
location” commits a Class C infraction.
      (5)   A person less than 18 who possesses or uses fireworks, without an
adult present and responsible, commits a delinquent act.
      (6)   Harming property with fireworks is a Class A misdemeanor.
      (7)   Causing serious bodily harm with fireworks results in a Level 6
Felony.
      (8)   Causing death with fireworks results in a Level 5 felony.
   (F)   Key provisions of state fireworks law. State law regulates fireworks
use, possession, and sale under I.C. 22-11-14. Below is a concise overview of
key provisions, with specific times, locations, age restrictions, and penalties
for violations. Local ordinances may impose stricter rules.
      (1)   Only individuals 18 years or older can purchase fireworks. Children
under 18 years old may use or possess fireworks only if an adult is present and
supervising.
      (2)   Fireworks may be discharged only on the user’s own property, the
property of someone who has given explicit permission, or a special discharge
location designated by the State Fire Marshal. Fireworks must be used at least
200 feet from any structure or vehicle.
      (3)   Fireworks are generally allowed from 9:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m. on
non-holiday days, unless restricted by local ordinances.
         (a)   Holiday hours: On Memorial Day, July 4, Labor Day, and New
Year’s Eve, fireworks are permitted from 9:00 a.m. to midnight.
         (b)   Protected periods (local ordinances cannot restrict these):
            1.   June 29 to July 3: 5:00 p.m. until two hours after sunset;
            2.   July 4: 10:00 a.m. to 12:00 a.m. (midnight);
            3.   July 5 to July 9: 5:00 p.m. until two hours after sunset; and
            4.   December 31 to January 1: 10:00 a.m. to 1:00 a.m.
         (c)   Holidays with extended hours (9:00 a.m. to 12:00 p.m.
(midnight)): New Year’s Day; Martin Luther King Jr.’s Birthday; Lincoln’s
Birthday; Washington’s Birthday; Good Friday; Memorial Day; Independence Day;
Labor Day; Columbus Day; Election Day; Veterans Day; Thanksgiving; and
Christmas.
      (4)   Types of fireworks.
         (a)   Consumer fireworks (1.4G): Includes sparklers, firecrackers,
bottle rockets, Roman candles, fountains, and novelty items like snakes and
party poppers. No permit is required for purchase or use by individuals.
         (b)   Section 8(a) fireworks: Includes sparklers, ground spinners,
cone fountains, wheels, and cylindrical fountains. Retail sales from temporary
stands require a permit.
         (c)   Special Fireworks (1.3G): Larger display fireworks require state
and federal permits from the United States Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco,
Firearms, and Explosives (ATF).
         (d)   Illegal Fireworks: Cherry bombs, M-80s, silver salutes, M-250s,
and quartersticks are banned and should be reported to the ATF at 1-888-283-
2662.
      (5)   No permit is required for individuals to purchase or use consumer
fireworks. Retailers, manufacturers, wholesalers, and distributors must obtain
a certificate of compliance from the State Fire Marshal. Retail sales from
temporary stands require a specific permit. Sellers must collect a 5% Fireworks
Public Safety Fee and 7% retail sales tax on gross retail income. Public
display fireworks require a permit from the State Fire Marshal and written
notice to residences within one mile at least 48 hours in advance.
      (6)   Safety requirements:
         (a)   Users shall use fireworks in a clear, open area away from
buildings, vehicles, and flammable materials;
         (b)   Users shall keep a fire extinguisher, hose, or bucket of water
nearby;
         (c)   Users shall not relight or handle malfunctioning fireworks; soak
them in water and dispose of them safely;
         (d)   Users shall never aim or throw fireworks at people or animals;
         (e)   Users shall store fireworks in a cool, dry place out of
children’s reach; and
         (f)   Users shall avoid using fireworks while under the influence of
alcohol.

   It shall be unlawful for any person to shoot, fire, or discharge any firearm
within the town limits, as provided herein. For purposes of this section,
FIREARM shall mean any weapon that is capable of expelling, designed to expel
or may readily be converted to expel a projectile by means of an explosion. The
term FIREARM shall include handguns, machine guns, rifles, shotguns, and all

§ 130.16 PROHIBITION.
   This subchapter shall not prohibit the discharge of firearms by certain
exempted persons, namely:
   (A)   Any legally appointed local or federal law enforcement officer acting
within the capacity of his or her authorized duty;
   (B)   Any person, who is legally authorized and licensed to own and possess
a firearm, is acting in self-defense, the defense of their dependents, or the
defense of a fellow citizen(s), if the circumstances are such that a reasonable
and responsible adult would determine that discharging the firearm is of
absolute and immediate necessity, and that no alternative and less dangerous
solution is feasible at the time, and in doing so, does not violate any local,
state, or federal laws;
   (C)   Any duly licensed hunters, provided all applicable hunting laws and
regulations are adhered to;
   (D)   Any person discharging a weapon at lawfully operated shooting range,
skeet range, or gun club; and
   (E)   This subchapter shall not be construed to restrict or otherwise
prohibit the legal possession, purchase or use of firearms in accordance with
applicable state law, the State Constitution or the United States Constitution.
(Penalty, see §
130.99)
§ 130.17 REGULATIONS.
   This subchapter only regulates the discharge of firearms within the town
municipal corporate boundaries and shall not be construed to regulate or
otherwise restrict the following:
   (A)   Firearms, ammunition, and firearm accessories;
   (B)   The ownership, possession, carrying, transportation, registration,
transfer, and storage of firearms, ammunition, and firearm accessories; and
   (C)   Commerce in and taxation of firearms, firearm ammunition, and firearm
accessories.

into the General Fund. An organization determined by the Internal Revenue
Service to be exempt under 26 U.S.C. § 501(c)(3) of the United States is exempt
from payment of the application fee.
(Prior Code, § 7-3-5-4)
§ 130.39 INVESTIGATION.

copy thereof to the Town Marshal, who shall immediately institute such
investigation of the applicant and the applicant’s business, employer,
contractor, principal, or supervisor as the Town Marshal shall deem necessary
for the protection of the public. If, as a result of such investigation, the
applicant is found to be disqualified under §
130.40, the Town Marshal shall report the same to the Town Clerk-Treasurer.
(Prior Code, § 7-3-5-5)
§ 130.40 LICENSE DENIED.

license, if the application or the Town Marshal’s investigation shows:

of the state or the United States will, or are likely to, occur;

inaccurate.
(Prior Code, § 7-3-5-6)

information in §
130.36(B)(1) to (B)(7), (B)(9), and (B)(11), together with a copy of the letter
issued under §
130.37. The license shall also contain a statement that other information, with
respect to the applicant, is available for inspection at the Clerk-Treasurer’s
office during regular business hours.
(Prior Code, § 7-3-5-7)

hours after its beginning time. Licenses issued under this subchapter cannot be
assigned, sold, or transferred in any manner, and do not authorize any person
other than the applicant to organize, participate, or conduct a parade.
(Prior Code, § 7-3-5-8)
§ 130.43 REVOCATION.
   (A)   Any license issued under this subchapter may be revoked by the Town
Council in accordance with this section.
   (B)   A license issued or renewed may be revoked for any of the following
reasons:
      (1)   Misrepresentation or any inaccurate statement contained in the
application;
      (2)   Misrepresentation or any inaccurate statement contained in a
renewal statement; and
      (3)   Any violation of this subchapter.
   (C)   Requests to revoke a license may be made by any person in a form to be
prescribed by the Town Council. Notice of the request, including a copy thereof
and the date, time, and place of the hearing thereon, shall be mailed by the
United States Mail, properly addressed and with sufficient first- class postage
affixed, to the licensee at his or her last known address at least five days
prior to the date set for the hearing, or shall be personally delivered to the
licensee by the Town Marshal in the manner of a summons at least three days
prior to the date set for the hearing. Notice of the request, including a copy
thereof and the date, time, and place of the hearing thereon, shall be mailed
by United States Mail, properly addressed and with sufficient first-class
postage affixed, to the person requesting revocation at his or her last known
address at least five days prior to the date set for the hearing, or shall be
personally delivered to the licensee by the Town Marshal in the manner of a
summons at least three days prior to the date set for the hearing, and shall
state that the person must be personally present for the request to be
considered.
   (D)   The request for revocation shall be heard by the Town Council at the
time, date, and place set forth in the notice. The person making the request
for revocation must personally appear at the hearing. If the Council finds, by
a majority affirmative vote of the members then present, that grounds exist for
the revocation of the license, it shall so declare and the license shall be
revoked and of no further force or effect. The licensee shall, within 24 hours
of such declaration, surrender the license to the Town Clerk-Treasurer or the
Town Marshal. If the person making the request for revocation is not personally
present, the request shall fail unless the Town Council, by majority
affirmative vote of those members present, adjourn consideration of the request
to another time, date, or place. A request which has twice failed to be
considered due to the absence of any required person shall be deemed denied.
(Prior Code, § 7-3-5-9)

§ 150.01 DEFINITIONS.
   For the purpose of this chapter, the following definitions apply unless the
context clearly indicates or requires a different meaning.
   ALLEY. A public way, whether or not paved, that is:
      (1)   Not a street or sidewalk;
      (2)   Publicly maintained and open to public vehicular traffic; and
      (3)   Less than two lanes wide.
   NON-MOTORIZED VEHICLE. Any machine or device that is designed, intended,
modified, or used to travel across, along, or over the ground that is:
      (1)   Capable of being occupied by or carrying one or more things,
animals, or human beings; and
      (2)   Designed, intended, modified, or used to move by means of power
derived from the physical effort of human beings.
   SIDEWALK. The part of a street between the curb lines, or the lateral lines
of a roadway, and the adjacent property lines intended for the use of
pedestrians.
   VEHICLE. Any machine or device that is designed, intended, modified, or used
to travel across, along, or over the ground that is:
      (1)   Capable of being occupied by or carrying one or more things,
animals, or human beings; and
      (2)   Designed, intended, modified, or used to move, in whole or in part,
by means of power derived from a source other than the physical effort of human
beings.
(Prior Code, § 4-1-1)
§ 150.02 SIDEWALKS.
   The following provisions apply to sidewalks within the town.
   (A)   Ice and snow. Each owner of real property within the town shall clear,
or ensure clearance, of any accumulation of ice or snow on sidewalks abutting
his, her, or its property within 24 hours of such accumulation.
   (B)   Obstructions. No owner shall place, or allow to become placed, any
obstruction upon a sidewalk abutting his, her, or its property that
substantially interferes with the normal movement of pedestrian traffic.
   (C)   Use. No person shall use the sidewalks of the town for any purpose
other than walking, running, or jogging. No person shall use the sidewalks of
the town for any race, contest, or game without the permission of the Town
Council. No person shall use the sidewalks of the town in a manner which
obstructs the safe and orderly flow of pedestrian traffic or which endangers
any person.
   (D)   Vehicles on sidewalks. No person shall place or allow to move on the
sidewalks of the town any motorized or non-motorized vehicle unless:
      (1)   The vehicle is a tricycle used by a child;
      (2)   The vehicle is a cart used by patrons of a grocery store;
      (3)   The vehicle is a wagon or cart used by a child;
      (4)   The vehicle is a wagon or cart used by parents to transport young
children; or
      (5)   The vehicle is a moveable seat used by a disabled person for
personal transportation.
(Prior Code, § 4-2-1) Penalty, see §
150.99
§ 150.03 ALLEYS.
   No person shall place, or allow to become placed, any obstruction upon an
alley that substantially interferes with the normal movement of vehicular or
pedestrian traffic.
(Prior Code, § 4-2-2) Penalty, see §
150.99

   (A)   The town may, from time to time, apply for and receive grants for the
construction of sidewalks. In accordance with the terms of such grants, these
ordinances, and applicable laws, the town shall construct said sidewalks under
the supervision of the Building Inspector and according to regulations
therefor.
   (B)   Upon installation, the owner of the real property on which such
sidewalk is built shall be responsible for maintenance and repair thereof.
(Prior Code, § 4-2-3)

juice of any plant or substance, upon the sidewalks, streets, alleys, or

(Prior Code, § 4-2-4) Penalty, see §

**** GENERAL PROVISIONS ****
§ 151.01 DEFINITIONS.
   For the purpose of this chapter, the following definitions apply unless the
context clearly indicates or requires a different meaning.
   MOBILE HOME. A thing which is:
      (1)   Designed, manufactured, modified, or remanufactured, for occupancy
as a dwelling or sleeping place; and
      (2)   Readily capable of movement by towing.
   MODULAR HOME. Any structure which is:
      (1)   Designed, manufactured, modified, or remanufactured, for occupancy
as a dwelling or sleeping place; and
      (2)   Whose major components or structures have been manufactured or
assembled away from the site on which it is being erected.
   RECREATIONAL VEHICLE. Any vehicle that is designed, manufactured, modified,
or remanufactured, for occupancy as a dwelling or sleeping place and which
contains or is able to accept a sink, toilet, stove, refrigerator, faucet,
shower, or bathtub.
(Prior Code, § 3-1-1)
§ 151.02 COUNTY BUILDING AND ZONING ADMINISTRATOR.
   In Montgomery County, the position of Building Commissioner is part of the
County Building and Zoning Department. This Department is responsible for
reviewing and issuing permits for building construction, demolitions, and
electrical inspections, as well as handling zoning, floodplain approvals, and
maintaining the county’s address database for unincorporated and incorporated
areas (excluding the City of Crawfordsville and its two-mile jurisdiction). The
Building and Zoning Department is overseen by the County Board of
Commissioners, which serves as the executive and legislative authority for the
county.

§ 151.30 PURPOSE AND SCOPE.
   The purpose of this subchapter is to provide minimum standards for the
protection of life, limb, health, environment, public safety, and welfare, and
for the conservation of energy in the design and construction of buildings and
structures. The provisions of this subchapter apply to the construction,
alteration, repair, use, occupancy, and maintenance and additions to all
buildings and structures, other than fences, in the town.
(Prior Code, § 3-3-1)

hereinafter described shall be deemed an UNSAFE BUILDING and a nuisance,
provided that such conditions or defects exist to the extent that the life,
health, property, or safety of any person is endangered:
      (1)   Whenever any door, aisle, passageway, or other means of exit is of
insufficient size, or is otherwise not arranged, to provide safe and adequate
means of exit in case of fire or other emergency;
      (2)   Whenever the stress in any material, member, or portion, due to all
dead and live loads, is more than one and one-half times the working stress or
stresses allowed for new buildings of similar structure, purpose, or location;
      (3)   Whenever any portion has been damaged by fire, earthquake, wind,
flood, or any other cause, to such an extent that the structural strength or
stability is:
         (a)   Materially less than it was before such event; and
         (b)   Less than the minimum requirements for new buildings of similar
structure, purpose, or location.
      (4)   Whenever any portion, member, or appurtenance is likely to fail,
become detached or dislodged, or to collapse and thereby risking injury to
persons or property;
      (5)   Whenever any portion of a building, or any member, appurtenance, or
ornamentation on its exterior is:
         (a)   Of insufficient strength or stability; or
         (b)   Is not so anchored, attached, or fastened as to be capable of
resisting a wind pressure of one-half that specified for new buildings of
similar structure, purpose, or location without exceeding the working stresses
permitted for such buildings.
      (6)   Whenever any portion has cracked, warped, buckled, or settled to
such an extent that walls or other structural parts have materially less
resistance to winds or earthquakes than is required for new buildings of
similar structure, purpose, or location;
      (7)   Whenever any part of the building or structure has a substantial
risk of collapse due to:
         (a)   Dilapidation, deterioration, or decay;
         (b)   Faulty construction;
         (c)   Removal, movement, or instability of any portion of the ground
necessary to support such building;
         (d)   Deterioration, decay or inadequacy of its foundation; or
         (e)    Any other cause.
      (8)   Whenever, for any reason, the building or structure, or any part
thereof, is manifestly unsafe for the purpose for which it is being used;
      (9)   Whenever the exterior walls or other vertical structural members
list, lean, or buckle to such an extent that a plumb line passing through the
center of gravity does not fall inside the middle one-fifth of the base;
      (10)   Whenever the building or structure, exclusive of the foundation,
shows 33% or more damage or deterioration of its supporting member or members,
or 50% damage or deterioration of its non-supporting members, enclosing walls,
outside walls, or coverings;
      (11)   Whenever the building or structure has been so damaged or has
become so dilapidated or deteriorated so as to become:
         (a)   An attractive nuisance to children; or
         (b)   Easily accessible to persons for the purpose of committing
unlawful acts.
      (12)   Whenever any building or structure has been constructed, exists,
or is maintained in violation of:
         (a)   Any specific requirement or prohibition made applicable by the
ordinances of this town; or
         (b)   Any law of this state or town relating to the condition,
location, or structure of buildings.
      (13)   Whenever any building or structure, regardless of whether it was
erected in accordance with all applicable laws and ordinances, has in any non-
supporting part, member, or portion less than 50%, or in any supporting part,
member, or portion less than 60%, of the strength, fire-resisting qualities or
characteristics, or weather-resisting qualities or characteristics required by
law in the case of a newly constructed building of similar area, height,
occupancy, and location;
      (14)   Whenever a building or structure, used, or intended to be used, as
a dwelling, is determined by any official charged with maintaining or
preserving public health to be unsanitary, unfit for human habitation, or in a
condition that poses a substantial risk of contributing to or causing illness;
      (15)   Whenever a building or structure, used or intended to be used as a
dwelling, is determined by any official charged with maintaining or preserving
public life or property from damage by fire to be a fire hazard;
      (16)   Whenever any portion of a building or structure remains after
demolition or destruction of the building or structure so as to constitute an
attractive nuisance to children or hazard to the public;
      (17)   Whenever any building or structure is so abandoned for a period in
excess of six months so as to constitute an attractive nuisance to children or
hazard to the public; or
      (18)   A mobile home which has been or is in the process of being placed,
constructed, or installed in violation of the code.
(Prior Code, § 3-4-4)
§ 151.59 CONSTRUCTION, ALTERATION, AND DEMOLITION.
   (A)   All reconstruction, alteration, repair, or demolition of buildings and
structures shall be performed in a good and workmanlike manner according to
standards and practices the accepted in the trade.
   (B)   The rules pertaining to construction, alteration, repair, or
demolition of buildings or structures promulgated by the Administrative
Building Council of Indiana shall be considered standard and acceptable
practice for all matters covered by this subchapter or orders issued pursuant
hereto.
(Prior Code, § 3-4-5) Penalty, see §
151.99
§ 151.60 UNSAFE BUILDING FUND.
   An Unsafe Building Fund is hereby established in the operating budget of the
town in accordance with the provisions of I.C. 36-7-9-14.
(Prior Code, § 3-4-6)
§ 151.61 VIOLATIONS.
   Any person, firm, or corporation, whether as owner, lessee, sublessee, or
occupant, who erects, constructs, enlarges, alters, repairs, moves, improves,
removes, demolishes, equips, uses, occupies, or maintains any building,
structure, or premises, or cause or allow any of the same to be done, in a
manner contrary to any of the provisions of this subchapter or any order issued
pursuant hereto commits a Class C infraction for each day such violation
continues.
(Prior Code, § 3-4-7) Penalty, see §
151.99
§ 151.62 HEARING.
   Assessment and calculation of costs of hearing pursuant to this chapter
shall be as follows.
   (A)   In the event a hearing is conducted as a result of the issuance of a
notice pursuant to the Unsafe Building Law, and the hearing results in a
finding that the subject property is in violation of this subchapter, the costs
of such hearing shall be assessed against and payable by the owner and/or
possessor such property.
   (B)   Costs of the hearing are deemed and designated to be those incurred
for initial inspection of the building, preparation of notice, costs of service
of notice, hearing officer per diem payment, attorney fee, post hearing
inspection fee, and any other costs directly associated with the hearing
procedure.
   (C)   Per diem allowance for the hearing examiner shall be $50.
   (D)   Attorney fees shall be that amount invoiced to the town by the
attorney for his or her services directly associated with the hearing.
(Prior Code, § 3-4-8)
§ 151.99 PENALTY.
   (A)   Any person violating any provision of this chapter for which no
specific penalty is prescribed shall be subject to §
10.99 of this code of ordinances.
   (B)   A person who violates §
151.16 shall be fined $100 for the first violation, and $200 for second and
subsequent violations involving the same recreational vehicle.
(Prior Code, § 3-2-2)
   (C)   If any person, firm, or corporation shall violate any of the
provisions of §§
151.30 to
151.44, or shall do any act prohibited herein, or shall fail to perform any
duty lawfully enjoined, within the time prescribed by the County Building and
Zoning Administrator, or shall fail, neglect or refuse to obey any lawful order
given by the County Building and Zoning Administrator in connection with the
provisions of §§
151.30 to
151.44, for each such violation, failure, or refusal, such person, firm, or
corporation, shall be fined in any sum not less than $10 nor more than $500.
Each day of such unlawful activity as is prohibited by the first sentence of
this division (C) shall constitute a separate offense.
(Prior Code, § 3-3-16)
